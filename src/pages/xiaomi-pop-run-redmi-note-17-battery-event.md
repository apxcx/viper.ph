Xiaomi Philippines held its fourth Xiaomi POP Run on October 24 at Parqal in Aseana City, turning a community race into a product-focused technology event. The official event registration page described the gathering as a larger edition for Xiaomi fans, runners, media, content creators and business partners. Instead of presenting a phone only through specifications, Xiaomi connected the event to an active-lifestyle use case. The race was also a local launch platform for the REDMI Note 17 Series, giving the brand a way to put battery endurance at the center of its messaging.

The 2026 event was powered by the REDMI Note 17 Series and highlighted a 10,000mAh battery. That capacity is materially larger than the batteries found in many mainstream smartphones, but battery life still depends on screen brightness, mobile data, GPS, camera use and software settings. A race environment makes those trade-offs easy to understand: participants may use phones for route information, photos, messaging and emergency contact throughout a long morning. The event therefore translated a technical claim into a practical scenario without proving identical endurance for every user.

Race Roster listed the event for Saturday, October 24, 2026, with the venue at Parqal Mall Aseana in Parañaque, Metro Manila. Online pre-registration is now closed, so the listing is useful as a record of the event rather than an open invitation to register. Xiaomi’s description says the POP Run has grown over three years from a community fun run into one of its recognizable annual brand events. That combination of organized activity, product demonstration and fan gathering reflects how electronics brands increasingly build local communities around devices.

For Xiaomi, the event also supports the REDMI Note 17 Series before buyers evaluate the usual questions: price, charging speed, weight, camera performance, software support and local warranty service. A 10,000mAh battery can be attractive for commuters, travelers, gamers and creators, but a larger cell may affect device thickness and weight. The POP Run does not replace independent testing or a full Philippine product listing. It does show how the local market is being used to connect smartphone hardware with visible, in-person experiences.
