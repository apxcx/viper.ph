OceanaGold Philippines has trialed Orica’s WebGen wireless blasting technology at the Didipio mine, describing the deployment as the Philippines’ first and Asia’s second commercial use of the system. The project replaces physical connections between explosives with a wireless initiation approach designed for underground work. It is a specialized change, but it illustrates how mining operators are using connected equipment to make difficult environments more predictable. The company says the technology can improve worker safety while reducing delays associated with conventional blast preparation.

![Sandvik underground loader representing automated mine operations](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/oceanagold-wireless-blasting-philippines/image-1.webp?v=202611110745)

WebGen is built around the ability to initiate blasts without a physical firing cable running through the work area. At Didipio, that can help remove people from locations where connections would otherwise need to be checked and maintained. The system is part of a broader operating model that treats information, automation, and monitoring as production tools rather than add-ons. In underground mining, where access is constrained and conditions can change quickly, fewer physical steps may also mean fewer opportunities for errors before a planned blast.

![tunnel boring machine representing excavation technology](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/oceanagold-wireless-blasting-philippines/image-2.webp?v=202611110745)

OceanaGold’s wider technology stack includes AutoMine, underground fiber and Wi-Fi, mobile equipment tracking, dispatch systems, drones, satellite monitoring, sensors, ventilation controls, and predictive maintenance. Those components matter because wireless blasting is not an isolated gadget. Its value grows when the mine can coordinate people, machines, schedules, and environmental data in one operating picture. The approach reflects a gradual move toward connected mining, where safety decisions and production planning increasingly depend on timely data from the working face. It also creates a clearer record for reviewing incidents and improving procedures.

![underground loader working in a mine representing digital operations](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/oceanagold-wireless-blasting-philippines/image-3.webp?v=202611110745)

The Philippine significance is less about copying one system and more about showing what local industrial technology adoption can look like. A connected mine still needs trained workers, strong procedures, regulatory oversight, and transparent safety reporting. Wireless initiation cannot remove every risk, and it does not replace engineering judgment. But the Didipio trial gives the country a concrete example of a global mining technology being adapted locally. If performance and safety results hold, similar tools could support more controlled operations across complex underground and surface projects.
