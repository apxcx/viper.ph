Samsung Philippines has named the 10 finalist teams in its 2026 Solve for Tomorrow competition, putting student-led technology ideas from science high schools across the country in the spotlight. The finalists include teams from Quezon City, Siaton, Caraga, Valenzuela, Lanao del Norte, Bayawan, Eastern Visayas, Tagbilaran and Baguio. Their projects now move into a mentoring and proof-of-concept stage before the final presentation. The announcement gives the local program a concrete face: young Filipino teams are being asked to turn community problems into workable, technology-based solutions.

![Solve for Tomorrow 2026 student technology graphic](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/samsung-solve-for-tomorrow-finalists-2026/image-1.webp?v=2026112106000)

The competition is designed for Grades 8 to 10 students in government-managed science high schools and is run with the Department of Education. Samsung said the 2026 cycle drew more than 500 submissions involving over 1,000 students from all 15 Philippine regions. That scale matters because it shows that technology education is not limited to the largest urban campuses. Teams are entering from different communities, where local concerns can shape projects around environmental sustainability, social change through tech and sport, and practical uses of artificial intelligence.

![Filipino student teams presenting technology ideas](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/samsung-solve-for-tomorrow-finalists-2026/image-2.webp?v=2026112106000)

The finalist list also shows the range of problems students are trying to address. Team SAGIP, CheckMite, Ligtas Skwela, HydroHero, From Pulp to Brine, KINAIYAHAN, KUHOL-LECTOR, AgriSpectra, VoltROOT and CanalCare will compete for three grand-winner slots. Samsung says the prizes total almost PHP 2 million in cash and devices for teams, schools and teacher advisers. More important for the teams, the next stage pairs their ideas with expert guidance so promising concepts can be tested beyond a classroom proposal today.

![Samsung Solve for Tomorrow student innovators](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/samsung-solve-for-tomorrow-finalists-2026/image-3.webp?v=2026112106000)

For the Philippines, the program is a reminder that the technology pipeline begins before college and before a startup is formed. Students who learn to define a problem, test an idea and explain its impact are building skills that schools, employers and communities can use. The finalists will still need evidence that their concepts work, but the national spread of the entries is encouraging. With mentoring ahead, Solve for Tomorrow can connect science education to real local needs while giving young innovators a clearer route from school project to prototype.
