The Batang Ligtas Act would place broader online child-safety responsibilities on platforms operating in the Philippines if it completes the remaining steps to become law. Inquirer Technology reported that the Senate ratified the bicameral conference committee report on September 30. The measure covers online sexual abuse and exploitation of children and the recruitment of minors into violent activities, updating enforcement tools for crimes carried out through digital services used by children, families and online communities across the country today now.

Under the proposed framework, covered platforms and technology companies would designate local legal representatives who can receive subpoenas, takedown requests and blocking orders. They would also be expected to cooperate with investigations and preserve relevant information. The bill introduces safety-by-design measures such as age verification and proactive blocking of child sexual-abuse material, shifting more responsibility toward prevention instead of relying only on user reports after harmful content appears on a service, improving prevention for users, investigators and responsible technology companies.

The measure would also give trained law-enforcement officers clearer authority to conduct undercover online operations, subject to warrant requirements and safeguards against police instigation. It would make the National Child Safety Office permanent and institutionalize the Philippine Internet Crimes Against Children Center, involving personnel from the Philippine National Police and National Bureau of Investigation. The proposed rules are intended to create formal channels for digital evidence, platform cooperation and coordinated investigations across agencies and technology providers serving Philippine users and families.

The bill had not yet become law in the source report; it still needed to complete the remaining steps after ratification. That distinction matters for users and platforms tracking compliance. If enacted, companies would need to prepare local legal contacts, preservation procedures, age-assurance controls and response processes while protecting due process and privacy. For families, the policy direction is clear: Philippine online services are being asked to build child safety into product design, not treat it as an afterthought in product design and platform governance.
