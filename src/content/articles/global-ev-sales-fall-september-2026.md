Global electric-vehicle sales fell 2 percent year on year in September to 2.15 million vehicles, ending six consecutive months of growth, according to data from UK-based Benchmark Mineral Intelligence reported by Reuters. The monthly decline hides a sharply uneven market. Europe posted its strongest month on record, but that increase was not enough to offset steep drops in North America and China. Year-to-date global volumes still rose 3 percent to 15.5 million vehicles, showing that a single weak month has not erased the broader expansion.

Europe supplied the clearest positive signal, with almost 600,000 EVs sold in September—10 percent above the previous record set in June. Reuters linked the performance to strong consumer demand and policy support. The result shows how incentives, emissions rules, model availability and charging infrastructure can influence regional adoption at the same time. A record month in Europe also changes the global comparison: growth is no longer evenly distributed, and markets with supportive policies can move in a different direction from regions where incentives are being withdrawn.

China remained the largest market but recorded an 18 percent decline to 1.11 million vehicles. Reuters said domestic weakness is prompting automakers to rely more heavily on exports, adding pressure to an already competitive global industry. North American sales fell 44 percent, the steepest monthly decline of the year, reflecting the strong comparison period before US federal EV tax credits expired at the end of September 2025. The fourth-quarter comparisons should become easier as the market moves beyond that unusual baseline.

The September numbers matter for the Philippines because global sales trends can affect vehicle imports, regional production decisions, model availability and pricing, even though the Reuters data does not establish a Philippine launch or promotion. Local adoption still depends on charging access, purchase costs, financing, policy and after-sales support. The wider lesson is that EV growth should be measured by region and use case rather than one worldwide headline. Europe’s record, China’s slowdown and North America’s policy effect point to a market that is expanding, but not uniformly.
