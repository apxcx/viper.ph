Smart Communications and Elveo Mobile have successfully tested direct-to-device satellite connectivity along the maritime route between Batangas Port and Calapan City in Oriental Mindoro. The trials used ordinary LTE smartphones connected directly to Elveo Mobile’s low-Earth-orbit satellites while vessels traveled the sea corridor. The companies demonstrated a possible way to extend basic mobile service beyond terrestrial cell sites. The route is significant because it carries daily passengers, cargo, and workers across an archipelagic area where connectivity can be difficult to maintain offshore.

![Smart and Lynk direct-to-device satellite connectivity equipment on a Philippine beach](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/smart-satellite-connectivity-philippines/image-1.webp?v=202611250847)

During the tests, teams sent text messages to contacts in Manila, received emergency broadcast messages, and loaded the official Smart and TNT websites. Those results show the trial was focused on practical communications rather than a showcase of specialized satellite handsets. Direct-to-device service works by linking compatible phones to satellites when a terrestrial network is unavailable. That distinction is important for users at sea. Smart said the tests will help it evaluate the technology’s capabilities and determine how it could be delivered while protecting customer experience.

![Smartphones and satellite connectivity equipment tested on a Philippine shoreline](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/smart-satellite-connectivity-philippines/image-2.webp?v=202611250847)

The companies positioned the technology as a complement to Smart’s existing network, not a replacement for cell towers and fiber. Satellite-to-phone links could add resilience for sea travelers, fishermen, maritime workers, and emergency responders in coastal or geographically isolated areas. Emergency broadcast support is particularly relevant during typhoons and other events that disrupt land-based infrastructure. The trial also builds on earlier Smart and Elveo Mobile pilots in Catanduanes, Pagudpud, Laoag, and Lobo, according to the announcement. It also gives operators another path to reach communities beyond normal coverage.

![Smart and Lynk satellite connectivity demonstration for mobile coverage](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/smart-satellite-connectivity-philippines/image-3.webp?v=202611250847)

No commercial launch date, pricing, or nationwide availability was announced in the release. The next step is evaluation: Smart must assess performance, capacity, device behavior, customer experience, and the regulatory and operational requirements for a broader service. For the Philippines, the trial matters because it tests connectivity on an actual inter-island route rather than in a laboratory. If the model proves reliable, direct-to-device satellites could become another layer in the country’s disaster-readiness and last-mile communications strategy. The announcement describes testing, not a promise of immediate service.
