DepEd distributed 1,441 Apple MacBook Neo laptops to public school teachers in Antipolo on November 20, placing the latest phase of its computerization program in the hands of educators from 62 schools. Education Secretary Sonny Angara and Rizal officials led the turnover at the Ynares Center. The event measures a much larger procurement: more than 230,000 teacher laptops are covered nationwide, including almost 170,000 MacBook Neo units. The rollout is expected to continue through March 2027 as the department works through its allocations.

![Antipolo teachers receive MacBook Neo laptops at the DepEd distribution event](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-macbook-neo-antipolo-teachers/image-1.webp?v=20261122082837)

The laptops are being paired with GuroXL, a professional development program intended to move the purchase beyond a hardware handout. In Antipolo, teachers practiced setting up the devices, navigating productivity applications and using digital tools to prepare instructional materials, manage class activities and assess learners. DepEd’s National Educators Academy of the Philippines, ICT Service and Bureau of Learning Delivery coordinate the program, while Apple Distinguished Educators support selected sessions. The procurement package includes this training, allowing teachers to build practical classroom routines alongside their new equipment.

![Teachers use MacBook Neo laptops during the DepEd distribution event](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-macbook-neo-antipolo-teachers/image-2.webp?v=20261122082837)

The program also shows why device distribution cannot be judged by unit counts alone. Teachers still need dependable connectivity, compatible classroom equipment and a clear support path when chargers, adapters or software cause problems. One Antipolo teacher told Inquirer that a lighter laptop could make presentations, grading, instructional materials and online student-journalism sessions easier, but said internet access had remained a concern with older devices. Those practical conditions will shape whether the laptops save time and improve lessons after the training room is left behind.

![DepEd teachers hold MacBook Neo laptops in the distribution venue](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-macbook-neo-antipolo-teachers/image-3.webp?v=20261122082837)

DepEd said the MacBook Neo package costs about P43,000 per unit, below the device’s P49,900 suggested retail price, because it includes delivery, built-in productivity software, professional development and a five-year warranty. The department also procured 3,646 units for administrative personnel, while about 62,000 laptops in the broader program will run Windows. Allocation remains limited: schools prioritize teachers who have not received government-issued laptops before. The Antipolo rollout therefore expands access, but its longer-term test is equitable coverage, reliable maintenance and daily classroom usefulness.
