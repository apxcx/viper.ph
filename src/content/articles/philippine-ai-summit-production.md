Philippine enterprise technology leaders gathered in Boracay for the ninth VST ECS CXO Tech Summit, where the central message was that artificial intelligence must move from experiments into everyday operations. The four-day, invitation-only event runs October 20 to 23 at Shangri-La Boracay under the theme “Rethinking IT: Powering the AI-Driven Enterprise.” VST ECS said the meeting brings together executives from banks, BPOs, healthcare, telcos, universities, manufacturers, retailers, energy, hospitality, logistics and government agencies to discuss practical deployment across Philippine industries and operations.

VST ECS President and CEO Jimmy Go said the timetable for simply testing AI tools has passed. In his framing, AI was a demonstration two years ago and a pilot last year, but leading organizations are now putting it into production for demand forecasting, software development and customer service. The shift does not mean every company is ready for the same projects. It means decision-makers are increasingly evaluating AI by business results, integration work and operational accountability rather than by demonstrations alone.

The summit also highlighted constraints that could slow deployment. Go pointed to global shortages in memory and storage that may delay hardware projects into 2027. Philippine businesses must additionally consider Executive Order 119 and its higher expectations around data residency and sovereignty when handling sensitive information. The event’s program includes technical sessions on cybersecurity and cloud infrastructure, along with speakers from major technology vendors and a CIO panel representing sectors including banking, healthcare, education and manufacturing nationwide and public services.

The discussion included a workforce question familiar to Philippine employers: whether automation will displace people. Go argued that AI will not simply replace talent, but that workers who use AI may replace workers who do not. That view places emphasis on training, governance and responsible adoption. For local companies, the summit’s practical takeaway is measured rather than automatic: production AI requires suitable infrastructure, secure data practices, capable teams and a clear business problem to solve for customers and staff in practice.
