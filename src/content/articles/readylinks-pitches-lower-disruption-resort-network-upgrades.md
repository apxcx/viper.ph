Trends & Technologies Inc. and connectivity company ReadyLinks are pitching a way for integrated resorts and hospitality businesses to modernize their networks without replacing all their existing infrastructure. At a September 10 forum at Solaire in Parañaque, the companies introduced the Smart Integrated Resort Solution. The approach is built around stronger connectivity, clearer visibility, simpler management and operational intelligence, with deployment designed to limit disruption for properties that must stay open. The companies presented the plan as an operational upgrade rather than a construction-heavy replacement project.

The proposal treats a resort’s current network investments as a foundation rather than an obstacle. Instead of a costly rebuild, the companies say operators can add capabilities around what is already installed and scale over time. That is particularly relevant for properties where gaming, hotel services, surveillance, retail, dining and events share the same digital backbone. Better visibility across properties could also help technical teams identify problems sooner and manage different environments more consistently. It could also make expansion easier when a property adds rooms, venues or connected services.

Downtime is a major concern in high-volume hospitality operations. Trends executive Mark Anthony Castillo cited an average potential loss of up to $15,000 a minute for Global 2000 companies in 2026, while noting the impact can be even greater for integrated resorts during peak periods. The point is not that every Philippine property faces the same figure, but that modernization plans must weigh service interruption, customer experience and revenue risk alongside hardware and software costs. A short outage can affect guests, staff workflows, security systems and payment points at the same time.

ReadyLinks CEO Larry Synstelien said the collaboration is intended to support future technologies while keeping transformation more subtle and cost-effective. For Philippine resort operators, the test will be whether the model can translate into measurable uptime, faster troubleshooting and smoother guest services. The initiative is sponsored content, so its performance claims remain a vendor proposition rather than an independent industry benchmark. Even so, it highlights how connectivity is becoming core operational infrastructure in hospitality. That makes network planning a business continuity issue, not only an information-technology project.
