The Philippines’ artificial intelligence ambitions are exposing a basic infrastructure question: can networks expand quickly enough to support them? Stratbase Institute President Victor Andres “Dindo” Manhit urged the government to accelerate telecommunications deployment as the country pursues its AI+ Infrastructure Master Plan for 2026 to 2033. His argument links AI adoption to practical capacity, including fiber routes, shared facilities, and reliable mobile coverage. Government digital services and technology-intensive industries will need connectivity that is more resilient and widely available than today’s uneven network footprint.

![PH Telecoms Need AI-Ready Infrastructure supporting image 1](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/philippines-telecom-ai-infrastructure/image-1.webp?v=202611260800)

Manhit said operators face a difficult investment problem because network expansion is increasingly capital-intensive while their ability to fund every project is limited. He proposed wider use of the Common Tower Policy, allowing carriers to share passive infrastructure, and publicly supported facilities that can reduce duplication. A unified underground conduit policy could also give multiple operators access to common corridors for fiber. Those measures would not replace private investment. They could make network expansion cheaper and faster in areas that remain commercially difficult to serve.

![PH Telecoms Need AI-Ready Infrastructure supporting image 2](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/philippines-telecom-ai-infrastructure/image-2.webp?v=202611260800)

The recommendation arrives as the Philippines promotes the Luzon Economic Corridor, the US-backed Pax Silica initiative, and a planned Super Intelligence, Knowledge, and Advanced Technology Corridor. Each effort depends on moving large volumes of data between businesses, public agencies, cloud platforms, and users. Without matching telecom capacity, AI projects risk concentrating in already connected business districts. Shared towers and fiber corridors would give government a way to complement private capital while also lowering regulatory and construction barriers that slow deployment.

![PH Telecoms Need AI-Ready Infrastructure supporting image 3](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/philippines-telecom-ai-infrastructure/image-3.webp?v=202611260800)

The policy discussion does not promise immediate upgrades or a specific funding package. It does clarify the infrastructure layer that will determine whether the Philippines can turn AI plans into everyday services. Updating building rules, simplifying permits, and reducing redundant fees could matter alongside new public spending. For households, schools, and smaller firms, the payoff would be broader access to digital services. For investors, stronger networks could make more locations viable for technology-intensive operations. The next test is whether agencies convert the recommendations into coordinated infrastructure projects.
