EVgo is previewing a next-generation direct-current fast-charging system capable of delivering up to 750 kW, with final design and deployment expected in 2027. The company says development and testing are underway at its Innovation Lab in El Segundo, California, with Delta Electronics support.**This is a future charging architecture, not a live 750-kW service for drivers today.**Electrek’s independent report likewise describes equipment that is not yet on EVgo’s network. No Philippine rollout, certification, price or local availability was announced in the sources reviewed.

The proposed system uses dynamic power sharing to adjust output among dispensers as vehicles plug in. EVgo says planned stations will use NACS connectors, easier cable handling and touchless payment, alongside hardware and firmware changes intended to improve reliability. A 750-kW ceiling describes the system’s maximum capability, not what every car accepts throughout a session.**Actual charging depends on the vehicle, battery state, temperature, settings and the number of vehicles sharing power.**Those real-world results remain to be demonstrated after deployment.

EVgo says the fastest-charging U.S. vehicles could charge in around 10 minutes, but that figure is a company estimate rather than a universal result. The release says charging time varies with the vehicle’s charging speed and settings, battery condition, ambient temperature and starting state of charge. Electrek notes that EVgo has not specified the battery-percentage interval behind the estimate.**Readers should not interpret the announcement as “all EVs charge in 10 minutes.”**The headline development is the planned architecture and power-management approach, not a current consumer promise.

The preview matters because high-power charging increasingly depends on managing the whole site, not simply increasing a single stall’s rating. Dynamic sharing could make a station more flexible as vehicles with different charging curves arrive, but that benefit still requires deployed testing. EVgo reports more than 1,200 fast-charging locations and 1.8 million accounts, figures that remain company-reported.**For Philippine readers, the relevant lesson is infrastructure design—not local access.**Neither source reviewed here establishes EVgo deployment, pricing or regulatory approval in the Philippines.
