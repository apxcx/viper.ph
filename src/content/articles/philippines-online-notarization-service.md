The**Supreme Court of the Philippines**has officially launched eNotarization services, allowing the public to have electronic documents notarized online. The service is designed to move a traditionally in-person legal step into a controlled digital process. The Court says electronic documents are signed and notarized through a secure Electronic Notarization Facility, under the administration of an electronic notary public. The rollout is a confirmed Philippine government service launch, not a proposal or pilot announcement. It gives citizens and organizations another route for handling eligible documents without beginning with a visit to a physical notary office.

To use the service, applicants must first register through the portal for the first batch of Supreme Court-accredited Electronic Notarization Facility providers. After registration, users may choose an electronic notary public from the list of commissioned professionals. That sequence matters because the service depends on accredited providers and commissioned notaries rather than an open marketplace of unverified online services. The Court has published the registration portal and a helpdesk contact for questions. Users should follow the portal instructions and confirm requirements before uploading documents or paying any service-related fee.

The launch could make notarization more convenient for people who work remotely, live far from service centers or need to process documents across locations. It may also reduce printing, scanning and repeated travel for transactions that qualify for electronic notarization. The announcement does not mean every document can automatically be notarized online, and it does not remove identity, consent or recordkeeping requirements. Applicants should check the accredited-provider process and the notary’s instructions for each document. The service’s practical value will depend on reliability, legal compliance and public understanding of its limits.

The eNotarization rollout adds another piece to the Philippines’ wider digital-government transition. Its long-term impact will depend on secure authentication, document integrity, privacy safeguards and clear remedies when a submission is rejected or a record needs correction. The Supreme Court has confirmed the service’s availability and registration path, but has not published a universal price list or a timetable covering every document type. For now, the verified development is straightforward: eligible electronic documents can enter a formal online notarization process through accredited facilities and commissioned electronic notaries.
