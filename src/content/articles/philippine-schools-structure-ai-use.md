Philippine schools are already using artificial intelligence, but educators say adoption needs to move beyond isolated experiments and become part of a stronger institutional strategy. BusinessWorld reported on a study by Instructure and The Fourth Wall that surveyed 207 higher-education and technical-vocational professionals in Metro Manila, Metro Cebu and Metro Davao. Presented at CanvasCon Philippines 2026, the findings describe a system where AI has entered classrooms and staff workflows, yet many institutions still lack consistent rules, training and support for learners outside traditional full-time programs.

Half of the educators surveyed said their institutions already had AI-literacy or digital-skills programs for students. About 40% reported staff-facing AI tools, AI integration in course content or AI-powered tutoring and learning assistance, while 18% said their institutions were still exploring AI without a current approach. The figures suggest that the debate is no longer whether AI has arrived in Philippine education. The harder question is whether schools can connect tools to learning goals, assessment, faculty practice and responsible-use guidance instead of leaving adoption to individual initiative.

Confidence scores reveal the gap. Educators rated their confidence that their institutions had a strategy linking AI to lifelong-learning pathways at 3.08 out of 5, while confidence in their own AI training averaged 2.87. Limited access to devices and paid tools was cited by 48% of respondents, and 38% cited a lack of training or guidance. The study also found that work and livelihood responsibilities and cost constraints can make lifelong learning harder. These barriers matter for remote, part-time and working learners who may benefit most from flexible AI support.

The study’s priorities include expanding access to devices and tools, setting clear rules for responsible AI use, building faculty and learner capabilities, integrating AI into curricula and lifelong-learning pathways, and measuring whether support reaches different student groups equitably. Researchers cautioned that the sample reflects educators and is not nationally representative of every Philippine institution. Even so, the findings offer a practical roadmap: schools need shared standards, training and metrics so AI benefits are not determined mainly by personal resources, improvisation or location.
