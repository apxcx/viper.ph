The Philippine Electric Vehicle Summit put the country’s expanding mobility market on display, bringing more than 15 brands to the SMX Convention Center in Pasay. The October event combined vehicle displays, test drives, charging equipment and sessions for owners and prospective buyers. Participating marques ranged from mainstream automotive companies to newer electric specialists. The breadth of the show reflects a market moving beyond a single early-adopter niche, with consumers now comparing vehicle sizes, charging needs, financing options and after-sales support.

![EV Summit organizers at the Driving ASEAN event](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/philippine-ev-summit-15-brands-2026/image-1.webp?v=20261029)

The growth figures cited by the Electric Vehicle Association of the Philippines are equally notable. CAMPI–TMA data showed electrified-vehicle sales reached 38,286 units from January through July 2026, a 136.4 percent increase from the same period a year earlier. Battery-electric vehicles rose 300.3 percent to 10,476 units. The broader vehicle market moved in the opposite direction, with member sales across all powertrains declining 10.2 percent, making the electric segment’s growth harder to dismiss as simple market expansion.

![Electric vehicle brands and models displayed at the Philippine EV Summit](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/philippine-ev-summit-15-brands-2026/image-2.webp?v=20261029)

Charging remains the practical test for that momentum. The Manila Bulletin report cited Department of Energy data showing 1,569 charging points and 258 accredited charging-station providers nationwide as of March 31. Summit displays covered home, commercial and industrial systems, as well as fleet solutions. Banks, insurers and financing companies were also present, underscoring that electric mobility depends on a wider ecosystem. Buyers need reliable charging access, predictable service and payment options as much as they need a capable vehicle.

![Electric vehicle test-drive lineup at the EV Summit](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/philippine-ev-summit-15-brands-2026/image-3.webp?v=20261029)

The summit’s message was therefore broader than a product launch. EVAP framed electric mobility as an energy, transport and economic issue, while government and industry speakers discussed safety, business integration, solar charging and common technology myths. More brands can improve choice and price competition, but adoption will depend on whether infrastructure keeps pace with sales. For Philippine buyers, the next useful comparison is not only range or acceleration; it is how a vehicle fits the charging network available at home, work and along regular routes.
