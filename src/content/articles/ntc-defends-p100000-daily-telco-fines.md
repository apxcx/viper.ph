The National Telecommunications Commission is defending**₱100,000-per-day penalties**imposed on Globe Telecom, Smart Communications and DITO Telecommunity over alleged mobile-broadband deficiencies. ABS-CBN News and Philippine Daily Inquirer reports said the penalties began on September 25 and would continue until the operators address service problems identified by NTC. The dispute concerns certain tested areas, including parts of Metro Manila, rather than a final finding that all three networks perform poorly nationwide. The case remains a regulatory enforcement dispute, with the operators and industry group challenging parts of the process and methodology.

NTC Deputy Commissioner Andres Castelar Jr. said show-cause orders give the companies an opportunity to explain their side, submit evidence and respond to the findings. The commission said a September revalidation found poor mobile-broadband signals in certain areas had not materially changed despite an opportunity for urgent corrective measures. The companies said they received their show-cause orders on September 28, three days after the stated fine start date. That timing is central to the due-process argument, but the reviewed reports do not establish a final adjudication or permanent license sanction.

BusinessWorld reported that the Philippine Chamber of Telecommunications Operators questioned the transparency of NTC testing methods, metrics, parameters and conditions. The reports present the regulator’s account and the industry’s objections separately; they do not resolve which methodology will ultimately prevail. The ₱100,000 figure is a daily penalty per operator, not a confirmed aggregate total. The findings also refer to alleged service deficiencies in specific tested locations, so readers should not interpret the enforcement action as proof of a nationwide network failure or assume that every subscriber experienced the same issue.

The next meaningful developments will be the operators’ formal responses, NTC’s review of submitted evidence and any decision on whether the penalties continue, change or are challenged further. For customers, the case highlights how network quality can depend on location, testing conditions, congestion and the service standard being applied. It also shows why transparent measurement matters when penalties affect major providers. Until the process concludes, the confirmed facts are the regulator’s daily-fine defense, the operators involved and the ongoing due-process and testing-standard dispute.
