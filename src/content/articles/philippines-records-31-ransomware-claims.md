Philippine organizations faced a heavier cybersecurity burden in the first eight months of 2026, with Check Point Software Technologies tracking 264 incidents among its local customers. BusinessWorld reported that 216 involved website defacement or information-system disruption, while 48 involved ransomware, breaches or data leaks. The report shows that visible disruption and severe compromise are not the same thing: government agencies recorded the highest overall volume, but financial institutions had the highest concentration of serious threats. The figures underline why organizations need layered controls rather than relying on a single perimeter defense.

Ransomware activity was especially notable. Check Point counted 31 public ransomware claims involving Philippine organizations through August, already above the 17 recorded during all of 2025 and the nine recorded in 2024. Fifteen groups posted claims in the eight-month period, including 10 appearing in the local dataset for the first time. The company said the increase looked more like broader participation and commoditized access than a completely new attack method. That makes prevention, identity protection and recovery planning important even for sectors that are not traditionally seen as prime targets.

Phishing remained a major route to compromise, with 2,386 alerts recorded in the period. Check Point said attackers were moving away from simply intercepting one-time text codes and increasingly using fake reward points and loyalty-program updates to solicit personal and payment information. It also tracked 24,875 data-exposure cases, with passwords and payment-card data making up much of the leaked information. These tactics make employee awareness and consumer caution inseparable from technical security, particularly when messages imitate trusted brands or familiar financial services.

The report also counted 1,194 social-media impersonation alerts, most involving companies, with Facebook accounting for roughly four out of five cases. Check Point said criminals were using AI mainly for social engineering, deepfake content, cloned voices and fraud rather than directly breaking into networks. Philippine organizations should therefore treat brand monitoring, stronger authentication, vendor controls and incident response as connected priorities. The figures describe Check Point customers and monitored claims, not every attack in the country, but they still show how quickly familiar scams can scale.
