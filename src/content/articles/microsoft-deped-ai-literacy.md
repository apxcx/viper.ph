Microsoft and the Department of Education have launched a professional development program designed to build artificial intelligence literacy among 2,000 Filipino public school teachers. The Microsoft Elevate for Educators initiative combines practical classroom training with guidance on responsible technology use and human oversight. Around 100 senior high school teachers joined the launch and hands-on session at Microsoft Philippines’ office in Makati. The program frames AI skills as part of teacher development, not as a replacement for educators, and puts judgment, adaptability, and accountability alongside tool familiarity.

![Teacher guiding students through a computer classroom exercise](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/microsoft-deped-ai-literacy/image-1.webp?v=20261114074000)

The partnership is intended to move beyond a single workshop. Participating educators will undergo instructor-led training, earn digital badges, and receive access to a Microsoft Elevate for Educators credential developed with ISTE+ASCD and aligned with its AI Literacy Framework. Audentes Technologies is supporting the delivery of the program with DepEd and Microsoft. That structure gives the initiative a defined path from introductory exposure to documented learning, while also creating a common baseline for teachers who will introduce AI concepts to public school students.

![Educator demonstrating digital learning tools in a classroom](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/microsoft-deped-ai-literacy/image-2.webp?v=20261114074000)

The program will continue with two virtual sessions in November 2026 and eight live learning sessions scheduled from January through May 2027. Microsoft also introduced Behind the Chat and the AI Classroom Toolkit, which offer classroom-ready material on digital privacy, critical thinking, and responsible online behavior. These resources matter because the educational challenge is not only how to prompt an AI system. Teachers also need to help students assess outputs, protect personal information, and understand when human review must override an automated suggestion.

![Students working at computers in a technology lab](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/microsoft-deped-ai-literacy/image-3.webp?v=20261114074000)

For Philippine schools, the initiative could help turn AI literacy from an isolated technology topic into a practical teaching capability. Its reach will depend on reliable connectivity, time for teacher training, and materials that work across schools with different resources. The staged schedule gives DepEd an opportunity to learn from early cohorts before expanding the program. It also gives school leaders a clearer way to connect AI adoption with curriculum goals, teacher judgment, privacy safeguards, and the everyday realities of public education.
