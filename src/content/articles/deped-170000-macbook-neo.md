The Department of Education has procured 172,654 Apple MacBook Neo laptops for public school teachers and administrative officers, turning a product announcement into one of the Philippines’ biggest education technology purchases. DepEd said 169,008 units are earmarked for teachers while 3,646 will go to administrative personnel. Apple CEO John Ternus described the agreement as the company’s biggest education deal ever for Mac during Apple’s quarterly earnings call. The rollout gives the government a concrete hardware program to pair with its broader computerization and classroom modernization agenda.

![Teacher training session with laptops](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-170000-macbook-neo/image-1.webp)

Deliveries are scheduled from November 2026 through March 2027, according to DepEd and reporting by Philippine outlets. The laptops were secured at roughly PHP43,000 each, a price that includes delivery, built-in productivity software, teacher training and a five-year warranty. DepEd said the package is below the Philippine suggested retail price for the base MacBook Neo. The warranty is notable because earlier government laptop purchases drew scrutiny over cost, processor age and value. This procurement therefore puts the contract terms and long-term support under almost as much attention as the device itself.

![MacBook Air laptop](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-170000-macbook-neo/image-2.webp)

The Apple order is part of a larger teacher-device program rather than a replacement for every existing computer initiative. DepEd said it has contracted 231,725 teacher laptops so far, including 62,717 Windows-based units. The department’s stated target is to expand access before the end of President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.’s term, but the numbers still leave a substantial gap against the country’s nearly 900,000 public school teachers. Distribution, inventory control, repair capacity and local connectivity will determine whether the devices translate into everyday classroom value.

![Students collaborating around laptops](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-170000-macbook-neo/image-3.webp)

For teachers, the practical test will be whether the rollout reduces the personal expense of buying or maintaining equipment and supports lesson preparation, assessment and communication. Apple also points to its Education Community platform, where Filipino educators have already shared curriculum-aligned lesson plans. That software and training layer could help the hardware deliver more than a one-time upgrade. It will still require transparent delivery reports, usable school networks and sustained technical support, especially in communities where connectivity and maintenance remain harder problems than the laptop purchase itself.
