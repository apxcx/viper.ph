Makati has prohibited drones and other unmanned aerial vehicles across the city for the whole of November as the Philippines prepares to host the 49th ASEAN Summit and related meetings. The city government said the restriction is intended to support smooth security and communications during the events. The decision places a temporary boundary around consumer, commercial and hobbyist drone operations at a time when airspace awareness, public safety and communications resilience are central to protecting delegates, transport routes and major venues.

![Unmanned aerial vehicle in flight](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/makati-drone-ban-asean-summit/image-1.webp)

The restriction covers the entire city rather than only the summit venues, reflecting the difficulty of policing small aircraft once they are airborne. The ASEAN meetings are scheduled for November 16 to 18 in Manila, and the Philippines is completing its year as chair before Singapore takes over in 2027. With United States President Donald Trump and other leaders expected to attend, authorities are treating unmanned aircraft as a security, communications and coordination issue rather than a conventional recreational activity.

![Drone technology displayed at an aerospace exhibition](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/makati-drone-ban-asean-summit/image-2.webp)

Drones can provide useful services, including photography, inspection, mapping and emergency response, but they also complicate protection planning around dense urban gatherings. An unidentified aircraft can force officials to pause movements, investigate operators or adjust communications procedures even when it carries no harmful payload. The Makati advisory therefore illustrates how cities are balancing the benefits of accessible aerial technology against the need for predictable airspace during major diplomatic events, heightened police deployments and crowded public gatherings across the capital during the summit period.

![Unmanned aircraft communications equipment](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/makati-drone-ban-asean-summit/image-3.webp)

For operators, the practical lesson is to check local advisories before flying and to obtain any required clearance well ahead of time. The month-long ban also signals that future large events may rely on more formal drone-registration, geofencing and enforcement systems. As unmanned aircraft become cheaper and more capable, Philippine cities will need rules that clearly distinguish harmless commercial work from risky flights while giving residents, businesses and public-safety agencies enough notice to plan around temporary restrictions well in advance.
