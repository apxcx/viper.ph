DOST-PAGASA is preparing to showcase a climate-monitoring tool that can help Philippine agencies see how El Niño affects different regions. The ENSO Assessment Simplified, or EASi, tool combines local rainfall records with El Niño, La Niña and neutral-phase data, then presents the results through interactive maps, charts and graphs. DOST says the system is designed for climate specialists, agriculturists and related agencies that need a faster way to assess local conditions and plan responses as the country faces a prolonged dry period.

![El Niño advisory and climate guidance from DOST-PAGASA](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/dost-el-nino-risk-tool-philippines/image-1.webp?v=202610314)

The tool is being presented against a difficult outlook. DOST has said a very strong El Niño could persist through the last quarter of the year and gradually ease in the first half of 2027. Secretary Renato Solidum Jr. said rainfall could fall by more than 60 percent for at least three months by March 2027, while temperatures rise. EASi cannot remove those risks, but it can help agencies compare regional patterns, identify vulnerable areas and connect forecasts with decisions about water, crops and public preparedness.

![La Niña impacts and preparedness guide from PAGASA](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/dost-el-nino-risk-tool-philippines/image-2.webp?v=202610314)

The technology is part of a wider group of Philippine responses to water and agricultural stress. Philippine Star reported a low-cost biochar filter developed in Zambales, Nutrio biofertilizer trials supported by Project SARAi and an automated furrow irrigation system from Central Luzon State University. These approaches address different points in the chain, from drinking water and soil productivity to the timing and amount of irrigation. Their value depends on whether communities, farmers and local governments can afford, maintain and apply them at scale.

![ENSO and El Niño climate effects guide for the Philippines](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/dost-el-nino-risk-tool-philippines/image-3.webp?v=202610314)

DOST-PAGASA plans to integrate EASi into the El Niño Online Platform so stakeholders can access the analysis more easily. DOST’s resilience work also includes site-specific farm advisories, climate information updates and solar-powered desalination and purification systems deployed in communities. The common thread is using local evidence before a hazard becomes a disaster. For the Philippines, a useful climate tool is not only a dashboard; it is a way to translate rainfall and temperature data into earlier choices about crops, water and emergency support.
