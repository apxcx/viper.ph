**Whoscall is expanding its anti-scam work from consumer awareness into business protection**through a new executive meet-up in the Philippines. The forum brought together representatives from government, banking and financial services, technology companies and other partners to discuss how organizations can detect impersonation earlier and protect customer trust across digital channels. The move reflects how scams now affect brands, platforms and service operations as well as individual users.

The Manila Standard reported that Whoscall presented Watchmen as an enterprise brand-protection layer. The solution is designed to monitor social platforms for suspicious content, fake advertisements and impersonation accounts, including threats assisted by**AI-generated content**. Earlier detection can help companies investigate abuse connected to their name and digital identity before customers are redirected to fraudulent pages or messages.

Whoscall also discussed its Anti Scam Intelligence SDK, which can place scam-protection capabilities inside mobile and web applications. That approach could be relevant to**banks, fintech firms, e-commerce companies, retailers and telcos**that want customers to receive warnings inside an existing digital journey. The article describes the SDK as a way for businesses to integrate intelligence into their own platforms instead of requiring users to switch to a separate security tool.

The executive event builds on a wider public-private anti-scam coalition involving government, companies, communities and consumers. Participants discussed technology integration, education, reporting and intelligence sharing, while the Whoscall mobile app remains the consumer-facing layer for suspicious calls, SMS messages and links. The initiative does not eliminate the need for users to verify transactions, but it highlights the growing role of**cross-sector digital trust**in Philippine online services.
