Philippine companies are expected to move from isolated artificial-intelligence experiments toward broader operational use in 2027, according to technology leaders cited by BusinessWorld. Executives from Dell Technologies, Hewlett Packard Enterprise and VST ECS said local firms are studying proven applications from earlier adopters while looking for gains in efficiency and competitiveness. The shift matters because businesses are no longer evaluating AI only as a novelty or productivity add-on. They are deciding where agents, automation and analytics can change everyday work without creating new costs or control problems.

![AI executive using an enterprise technology interface](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/philippine-business-ai-adoption-2027/image-1.webp?v=20261031)

The infrastructure picture is becoming part of that decision. The report said 32% of Philippine businesses used AI in 2026, up from 21% in 2025, while data centers and hyperscalers expand the computing supply available to local customers. Leaders still pointed to power, cooling and licensing expenses as practical constraints. Older systems and fragmented data can also make deployment harder. For smaller companies, the next phase may depend less on building a model and more on choosing services that fit existing systems and budgets.

![AI concept graphic for enterprise technology](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/philippine-business-ai-adoption-2027/image-2.webp?v=20261031)

Enterprise AI agents were another focus of the discussion. HPE said Philippine companies are already developing and testing agents, but warned that governance must keep pace with experimentation. An agent that can act across software systems needs clear permissions, reliable data and a way to explain or reverse important decisions. Cybersecurity also becomes more important when automated tools can access business records. These controls are not separate from adoption; they determine whether companies can expand useful pilots without exposing customers, employees or critical operations to avoidable risk.

![Philippine AI adoption explainer graphic](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/philippine-business-ai-adoption-2027/image-3.webp?v=20261031)

The country’s opportunity therefore comes with a systems challenge. Technology leaders said businesses need better data readiness, cybersecurity, governance and modern IT infrastructure alongside new AI tools. Executive Order No. 119, which establishes a data-residency framework for government data, was cited as one factor that could attract hyperscaler and data-center investment. For Philippine firms, the competitive question in 2027 will be practical: can they connect AI to trusted information, affordable infrastructure and accountable workflows quickly enough to improve service before regional rivals pull further ahead?
