The global move toward electric vehicles looks inevitable in the long term, but the transition will not happen at the same speed everywhere. Reuters, citing projections from risk-management firm DNV, reported that battery-electric vehicles could eventually take more than 90 percent of passenger-car fleets in regions including China, Europe and Southeast Asia by 2050. The forecast is about vehicles already on the road, not only annual sales. That distinction matters because replacing a global fleet takes years after consumers begin buying more new EVs.

China is already the largest force in the shift. DNV estimates that roughly**37.5 million passenger EVs**are in use there, more than the rest of the world combined. The total could exceed 165 million by 2035 and 360 million by 2050. China’s manufacturing base, integrated battery supply chain, charging network and policy support have helped reduce ownership costs. The result is a faster change in both sales and fleet inventory, while combustion-engine cars are projected to decline substantially over the same period.

Europe is also expected to move quickly, with 80 percent or more of passenger fleets projected to be electric by 2040. Its battery-electric inventory could rise from about 12.5 million today to nearly 70 million by 2035 and 177 million by 2050. North America is on a slower track because policy reversals, charging debates and relatively cheap gasoline have weighed on adoption. Even so, DNV projects North American EV inventory rising from about 8 million cars today to nearly 86 million by 2040.

India shows why headline global forecasts need regional context. Its internal-combustion passenger fleet is still expanding and could reach about 165 million in the late 2040s, while limited charging and cost concerns delay a wider EV surge. Electric two-wheelers are growing faster and may help build infrastructure for later passenger-car adoption. For Southeast Asia, including the Philippines, the lesson is that**EV growth will depend on charging, prices, policy and supply**as much as on global automaker targets. The destination may be electric, but the route will remain uneven.
