The Philippines recorded 27,767 registered electric vehicles in 2025, almost five times the 6,697 units registered a year earlier, according to the Philippine Statistics Authority. The figure comes from Land Transportation Office registration data included in the government’s Compendium of Philippine Environment Statistics. Electric vehicles still represent a small share of the country’s 14.79 million registered vehicles, but the pace of increase is significant. It shows that EV adoption is moving beyond pilot fleets and early enthusiasts into a market that manufacturers, dealers and policymakers can measure more clearly.

![Electric vehicle charging station visual 1](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/philippine-ev-registrations-2025/image-1.webp?v=2026112008000)

The same data set recorded 33,139 hybrid vehicles in 2025, up 88.4 percent from 17,589 in 2024. That growth gives Philippine buyers more electrified choices while charging infrastructure remains uneven across the country. Battery-electric vehicles require dependable home, workplace or public charging, whereas hybrids can rely more heavily on existing fuel networks. The two categories will likely develop together as consumers compare acquisition cost, driving range, maintenance and resale value. Registration growth is encouraging, but it does not by itself show whether ownership is convenient outside major urban centers.

![Electric vehicle charging station visual 2](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/philippine-ev-registrations-2025/image-2.webp?v=2026112008000)

The numbers also create a clearer planning signal for charging operators and local governments. More vehicles mean demand for strategically placed stations, stronger grid coordination and standards that make different vehicles and chargers work together. Developers will need to understand where drivers travel, how long vehicles stay parked and whether commercial sites can support higher electrical loads. The national road network reached 35.66 thousand kilometers in 2025, according to the PSA release, so EV policy must connect vehicle growth with the realities of long-distance travel and regional infrastructure.

![Electric vehicle charging station visual 3](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/philippine-ev-registrations-2025/image-3.webp?v=2026112008000)

For the Philippine automotive industry, the near-fivefold increase is an opportunity but not a guarantee of mass adoption. Buyers still weigh prices, financing, battery warranties, repair coverage and the availability of trained technicians. Automakers can support the transition by offering transparent ownership costs and building service networks alongside vehicle sales. Government agencies can help by maintaining consistent registration rules, improving charging standards and publishing better data. The latest count establishes a useful baseline: EV demand is growing quickly, and the next challenge is making the ecosystem dependable.
