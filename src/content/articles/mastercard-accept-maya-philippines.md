Mastercard has introduced Mastercard Accept, a phone-based payment feature designed to let eligible debit and prepaid cardholders receive payments through banking or payments apps they already use. Instead of relying on a separate point-of-sale terminal, a seller can select the acceptance option, enter an amount, and present a smartphone for a contactless tap or QR-code payment. Mastercard announced the product on November 17 and positioned it as a way to make digital commerce more accessible to informal sellers, freelancers, and small businesses.

![Mastercard Accept Targets Small Sellers supporting image 1](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/mastercard-accept-maya-philippines/image-1.webp?v=20261118080000)

The Philippine angle comes through Maya Bank, which Mastercard named among its issuing and acquiring partners. Maya Group President Shailesh Baidwan said the company aims to bring Mastercard Accept to the Maya Business app, particularly for home-based sellers and independent service providers who may not need traditional payment terminals. The announcement describes a planned integration rather than a completed nationwide rollout. Availability, eligibility, and final customer experience will depend on implementation by Maya and the relevant payments partners in the Philippines.

![Mastercard Accept Targets Small Sellers supporting image 2](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/mastercard-accept-maya-philippines/image-2.webp?v=20261118080000)

Mastercard Accept works by extending an existing banking relationship. A cardholder opens a supported app, chooses to accept a payment, enters the required amount, and lets the payer tap a card or scan a displayed QR code. Mastercard says the solution uses the security and compliance standards already attached to its debit and prepaid propositions. For microbusinesses, the attraction is lower equipment cost. For banks, transaction history could create a path to broader merchant services as local sellers grow over time.

![Mastercard Accept Targets Small Sellers supporting image 3](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/mastercard-accept-maya-philippines/image-3.webp?v=20261118080000)

If Maya brings the capability to Philippine users, it could fit a market where many small sellers operate through social platforms, home businesses, and service work rather than formal storefronts. The opportunity is not only faster payment acceptance. Sellers will still need clear fees, settlement timing, fraud controls, dispute processes, and reliable connectivity. Mastercard’s announcement sets out the technology and partner direction, while the value for Filipinos will be determined by the local launch terms and how simply everyday sellers can use it.
