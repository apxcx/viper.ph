GCash has introduced GTourist, a temporary account designed to let foreign visitors make payments while traveling in the Philippines. The account lasts 30 days and is initially available to US nationals aged 18 and above, with GCash saying more countries will follow. Visitors can register with a non-Philippine mobile number, passport, and selfie. The service places a familiar local wallet inside the tourism journey, giving eligible travelers another way to pay merchants without relying entirely on cash or arranging a Philippine mobile account.

![GCash app account screen for foreign visitors](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/gcash-gtourist-foreign-visitors-qr-payments/image-1.webp?v=202611260923)

Registration requires roaming to receive a one-time PIN, and the account only activates after the visitor arrives in the Philippines with location sharing turned on. GCash’s help guidance says applicants can receive the verification result by SMS within 24 hours. Fully verified users can scan QR codes to pay merchants. The setup is therefore not an anonymous travel wallet. It uses identity checks and location controls, while leaving the final account limits and costs dependent on the service terms and partner arrangements.

![GCash mobile payment app on a smartphone](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/gcash-gtourist-foreign-visitors-qr-payments/image-2.webp?v=202611260923)

GTourist users can top up through linked foreign credit or debit cards, international banks, or PayPal. Partner-set exchange rates apply, and GCash has not published fees or wallet limits specific to the product in the announcement. When the 30-day period ends, the account is deactivated. Visitors can request that remaining funds be sent to a US bank account, with conversion to US dollars and possible transfer fees. Those details make the service closer to a temporary payments bridge than a permanent digital-bank relationship.

![GCash logo and mobile wallet payment](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/gcash-gtourist-foreign-visitors-qr-payments/image-3.webp?v=202611260923)

The launch fits a wider push to make tourism transactions more digital. GCash has previously worked with Visa and Mastercard on ways for visitors to fund or connect cards to its wallet, and it signed a Department of Tourism partnership to help accredited tourism businesses go cashless. Foreign arrivals reached 2.96 million from January to mid-June 2026, according to the cited report. GTourist’s next challenge will be adoption: travelers must understand verification, exchange rates, and account expiry before choosing QR payments during their trips.
