TP in the Philippines has expanded its**AI literacy training**under the Skills Training to Employment Program, or TP STEP, according to Manila Standard. The initiative aims to prepare job applicants for changing demands in customer service and business-process operations. As companies introduce automation and AI-assisted tools, training programs can help applicants understand how these systems work, where human judgment remains essential, and which digital skills are becoming more valuable in the workplace.

AI literacy is broader than learning to use a chatbot. It can include data handling, prompt design, quality checking, privacy, cybersecurity, and the ability to recognize when an automated answer requires human review. For applicants, these skills may improve readiness for roles that combine communication with digital tools. For employers, structured training can reduce the risk of deploying systems without enough oversight, documentation, or understanding of how errors affect customers.

The Philippine BPO sector is a major source of jobs and export revenue, but it is also exposed to automation pressure. Training can help workers move toward more complex tasks such as escalation handling, specialized support, compliance, analysis, and AI-assisted operations. That transition will require clear pathways from lessons to employment, as well as assessments that show whether applicants can apply skills in real customer or business scenarios rather than only complete a short course.

TP’s expansion also reflects a wider debate about how AI should change work in the Philippines. The strongest programs will combine technical lessons with human oversight, communication, ethics, and worker protection. AI adoption is not automatically a job replacement story; its impact depends on how companies redesign roles and invest in people. For Filipino applicants, accessible training tied to real vacancies could make the difference between being displaced by new tools and moving into higher-value work alongside them.
