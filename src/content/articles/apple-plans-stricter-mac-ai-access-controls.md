Apple says it will introduce**additional macOS controls**around Full Disk Access, a permission that can expose files, mail, messages and browsing history on a Mac. In an October 2 developer-newsroom note, Apple tied the change to the growing capability and autonomy of AI agents. Reuters and Engadget independently reported the announcement in the same news window. Apple said users who genuinely want to grant this level of access will need to take very explicit action. The company did not announce a finished macOS release, version, rollout date or final interface.

The planned change addresses a broad app-permission issue rather than creating a separate AI-only permission category. Apple said some developers are using Full Disk Access without users’ full knowledge and understanding, while increasingly autonomous agents raise the stakes because they can act across files and services. The announcement does not establish that any specific agent bypassed permission controls. Reuters reported Meta’s response about Muse access being opt-in and requiring Full Disk Access plus a Messages connector; that response should not be presented as proof of unauthorized message reading.

For Mac users, the important distinction is between a warning about future safeguards and a completed security update. Apple has not disclosed the final technical implementation, whether existing grants will be revisited or how developers must change their products. The reviewed sources also do not say that Apple will make AI-agent access impossible or that the controls apply only to AI software. Full Disk Access remains a general macOS permission, with AI agents identified as a growing risk rather than the sole subject of the policy.

There is no Philippine launch, pricing, distribution or regulatory action in the announcement. Local Mac owners should therefore treat this as an international Apple security-policy update and continue reviewing broad permissions carefully until Apple publishes implementation details. Developers should watch Apple’s release notes and testing guidance before promising compatibility, and users should review permission prompts before installing agent software. The confirmed news is that stronger user action is planned for Full Disk Access; the timing, design, affected macOS versions and migration requirements remain unknown.
