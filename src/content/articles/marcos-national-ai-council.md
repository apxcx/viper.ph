President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. has created the National AI Council through Executive Order 131, giving the Philippine government a formal body to coordinate artificial intelligence policy. The order responds to AI’s growing effect on business, jobs, education, media, and public services. The Palace said the council will facilitate and monitor implementation of the national AI framework, bringing agencies and other stakeholders into a shared process. For the Philippines, the move shifts AI governance from separate projects toward a more coordinated national program with clearer accountability and direction.

![Government technology conference audience discussing innovation](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/marcos-national-ai-council/image-1.webp?v=20261116081200)

The framework described in the order rests on four connected elements. Ethics and principles require human-centered, transparent development. Standards and assurance call for technical testing and evaluation. A risk-based policy toolkit allows safeguards to match the context and potential harm of each use case. Enabling resources cover institutions, research, infrastructure, capabilities, and demand generation. Together, these pillars give regulators a way to discuss AI safety without treating every application as equally risky or applying a single rule to every sector.

![Science and technology partnership conference delegates](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/marcos-national-ai-council/image-2.webp?v=20261116081200)

The council also puts workforce preparation at the center of the government’s approach. The Department of Science and Technology has been advancing the National AI Strategy for the Philippines through 2028, while officials have warned that earlier projects were energetic but scattered. Upskilling and reskilling will therefore matter alongside regulation. Universities, agencies, companies, and civil-society groups will need practical programs that help workers understand AI tools, redesign tasks, and identify risks before systems are deployed in sensitive settings across government and industry.

![Students working in a computer science classroom](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/marcos-national-ai-council/image-3.webp?v=20261116081200)

Businesses will be watching how the council turns policy into usable guidance. President Marcos has separately urged Southeast Asian companies to make AI more affordable and accessible to micro, small, and medium enterprises. That makes implementation important for local firms that may lack legal, technical, or compliance teams. If the council can connect safeguards with training, infrastructure, and practical support, the Philippines can pursue productivity gains while maintaining public trust and giving workers a clearer path through the transition for workers and businesses nationwide.
