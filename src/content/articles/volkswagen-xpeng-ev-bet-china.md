Volkswagen’s partnership with Chinese electric-vehicle maker Xpeng was intended to help the German automaker develop cars faster for China. Reuters’ review of company documents and interviews suggests the alliance’s first major product, the ID. UNYX 08 electric SUV, is struggling to match expectations. The vehicle averaged 267 monthly sales between its April launch and August, according to ThinkerCar data, far below Volkswagen’s original target of 3,622 per month. The result highlights the difficulty of translating a technology partnership into market success.

The UNYX 08 faces competition from software-rich Chinese electric SUVs that offer more features at similar prices. Reuters reported that Volkswagen launched the vehicle at about $34,260, while rivals such as Xiaomi’s YU7 and Zeekr’s 7X posted much higher sales during the same period. Analysts told Reuters that Chinese buyers increasingly expect advanced sensors, fast charging and connected software, making brand heritage less persuasive when local products deliver more technology for less money, with faster updates and charging capabilities.

Distribution added another obstacle. Volkswagen largely sold the UNYX 08 through a newer EV-focused dealer network run by Volkswagen Anhui instead of its more than 3,000 established dealerships with FAW and SAIC. Reuters reported that the smaller footprint limited consumer awareness, although Volkswagen said the network had expanded to 166 outlets and was working with selected FAW-Volkswagen dealerships in major Chinese markets. The company also said its Xpeng partnership produced a local electric architecture in a record 18 months.

The challenge extends beyond one model. China once contributed nearly half of Volkswagen Group profits, but now represents about 10%, while the company’s share of Chinese passenger-car sales has fallen sharply. Volkswagen is still preparing the Xpeng-linked UNYX 09 sedan, with ambitious internal targets despite the first model’s weak start. The episode shows why global automakers need more than local technology: they also need competitive pricing, software execution, distribution and a product strategy that fits rapidly changing consumer expectations in China.
