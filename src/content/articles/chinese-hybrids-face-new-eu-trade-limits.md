Chinese automakers may face tighter access to the European Union after a proposed trade arrangement shifts attention from tariff relief to import limits. Reuters reported that Chinese hybrid exports to the EU’s 27 member states tripled to**$7.5 billion**in the first half of 2026, from $2.5 billion a year earlier. Plug-in hybrids gained ground after the EU imposed additional tariffs on Chinese-made fully electric vehicles, because hybrids were exempt and could offer longer range where charging networks remain uneven.

SAIC, BYD and Chery accounted for more than 90 percent of Chinese hybrid sales volume in the EU, according to Jefferies data cited by Reuters. Chinese brands also captured a record 15 percent share of Europe’s electric-vehicle market in the second quarter, up from 9.5 percent in the first quarter of 2024. Plug-in hybrids made up more than a quarter of the segment. The figures show how quickly Chinese manufacturers have expanded beyond low-cost electric cars into mixed powertrains.

The China-Europe arrangement is expected to cut imports of several million cars over four years, although Brussels and Beijing have not released the final operating rules. China’s Commerce Ministry said the deal would allow continued export growth and guarantee China a quota no smaller than that granted to other import sources. Analysts described the situation as “tariff risk off, quota risk on,” because price floors, volume caps and exemptions still need technical negotiation. Automakers must plan around policy details that remain unsettled.

The uncertainty could encourage Chinese manufacturers to build more vehicles inside Europe instead of relying entirely on shipments from China. Changan said local production could begin early next year, while analysts said localization may become more attractive if import access is constrained. That would move competition closer to European factories and suppliers. For the global EV industry, the dispute shows that vehicle technology, trade policy and industrial investment are becoming inseparable as hybrids and fully electric models compete for market share.
