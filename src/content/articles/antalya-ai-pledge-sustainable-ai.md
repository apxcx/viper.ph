The COP31 presidency and the International Telecommunication Union have launched the Antalya Artificial Intelligence Pledge, putting the environmental cost of AI alongside its promise for climate action. The pledge asks governments, companies, research institutions, and civil-society groups to support measurable steps for low- and zero-emission power, sustainable infrastructure, and responsible AI systems. Its launch reflects a growing policy reality: expanding models and data centers can help solve environmental problems, but they also consume significant energy, water, hardware, and network capacity.

![Antalya AI Pledge sustainable AI event display](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/antalya-ai-pledge-sustainable-ai/image-1.webp?v=20261118085000)

The ITU’s framework is organized around four areas. It covers sustainable power and construction for AI infrastructure, responsible design and management across the system lifecycle, assessment and reporting of environmental impacts, and financial and technical support for public AI climate action in developing countries. The pledge also points to green data-center requirements, AI skills, and computing capacity. These details give the initiative a broader scope than a simple promise to purchase renewable electricity for technology facilities and regional cloud campuses.

![Antalya AI Pledge commitments for climate-conscious AI](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/antalya-ai-pledge-sustainable-ai/image-2.webp?v=20261118085000)

Reuters reported that the pledge attracted 90 endorsements at launch, including nine countries and Alibaba, while major US technology companies such as Meta, Microsoft, Google, and Nvidia had not signed yet at that point. The participation gap highlights the challenge of voluntary standards. Hyperscale operators control much of the infrastructure driving AI demand, yet they also face pressure from investors, regulators, and communities concerned about emissions, power requirements, and new data-center construction, grid demand, local water use, and nearby communities.

![COP31 Türkiye Antalya venue sign](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/antalya-ai-pledge-sustainable-ai/image-3.webp?v=20261118085000)

For technology companies serving the Philippines, the pledge offers a useful benchmark even though it does not immediately change local rules. Businesses can expect growing questions about cloud providers, energy sources, hardware reuse, emissions reporting, and the resilience of AI infrastructure. The ITU says progress will be tracked through 2030, giving governments and companies time to translate broad commitments into comparable measures. The success of sustainable AI will depend on transparent reporting and practical investment, not only on endorsements announced at climate conferences.
