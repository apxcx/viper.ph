Ericsson says Filipino 5G smartphone users are showing strong interest in**guaranteed network performance**during critical moments. In a study released on September 30, 60% of respondents in the Philippines identified situations when consistent service matters, compared with 40% globally. The finding points to demand for more predictable connectivity during activities such as streaming, cloud work and emerging AI use. It does not mean every Philippine network currently provides assured 5G service. Ericsson’s survey is a market-demand signal, not a government measurement of nationwide coverage, speed or reliability.

The study also found that 36% of Filipino 5G users would pay a premium for guaranteed quality of service. Ericsson estimates that differentiated connectivity could create up to two months of additional average revenue per user for providers, but that is a commercial forecast rather than observed Philippine telco revenue. The global research surveyed 43,000 smartphone users across 27 markets, including about 34,000 active 5G users. The Philippine sample included 1,500 consumers, with 900 active 5G subscribers, and the fieldwork took place from June to August 2025.

Ericsson links the demand to changing mobile habits and the growth of artificial intelligence on smartphones. It projects daily multimodal AI engagement among Filipino smartphone users to rise from 16% currently to 32% by 2030, while 44% of AI activity is expected to happen on the go. The report says response time has twice the effect on network satisfaction among current smartphone AI users compared with other 5G subscribers. Those figures describe Ericsson’s projections and survey findings, not a newly launched Philippine AI or premium-connectivity product.

The report’s business question is whether operators can turn that appetite into clearly defined service tiers. In the Philippines, 36% of respondents prefer buying differentiated connectivity from their provider, while 25% are open to capabilities delivered through third-party apps. Ericsson points to programmable network tools such as Quality on Demand as one possible enabler. No operator announcement, price, rollout date or nationwide guarantee accompanied the study. For local readers, the immediate story is the gap between demand for assured 5G and the absence of a confirmed mass-market offer.
