The Department of Information and Communications Technology is investigating**slow and intermittent internet**reported in parts of the Philippines on October 2, according to GMA News. Advisories cited by the report pointed to affected submarine-cable links between the Philippines and Singapore and between the Philippines and Hong Kong. DICT Secretary Henry Aguda said the cause was not yet known and could involve a natural fiber cut or equipment failure. Sabotage was only one possibility under investigation. The reports describe a disruption, not a confirmed nationwide blackout or a completed fault diagnosis.

GMA said Converge, PADECO and the UP Diliman Network Help Desk had issued advisories, while providers used alternative capacity as restoration work continued. Aguda said connectivity was not completely lost and that PLDT had told him it had enough provision to cover an estimated 300 gigabits per second of lost capacity. That figure is an official account, not an independent measurement. PhilSTAR Tech separately reported higher latency and intermittent overseas performance, but cautioned that available monitoring did not identify a specific cable or prove a physical fault.

The reported symptoms matter because international traffic can remain reachable while latency rises or selected services become unreliable. PhilSTAR Tech said CableStatus measured about 177 milliseconds on the Philippines-Singapore route, compared with a recent baseline near 53 milliseconds from 28 RIPE Atlas anchors. That telemetry does not establish the exact cause, and user reports on outage trackers do not independently prove scope. The reviewed reports also did not establish a restoration deadline, named repair vessel or confirmed cable system, so those details should not be inferred.

For users, the immediate story is service variability rather than a confirmed collapse of Philippine connectivity. Businesses and households may see different effects depending on provider routing, destination and use of alternate capacity. DICT and network operators still need to identify the affected segment and publish a restoration update before the incident can be described more precisely. Until then, readers should treat claims of sabotage, a nationwide blackout or a final repair timetable as unverified. The strongest confirmed point is that officials were investigating cross-border cable-related disruption while connectivity continued.
