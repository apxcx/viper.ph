SpaceX is targeting**Starship Flight 14**for its first orbital test on Monday, September 28, pending regulatory approval. The company lists a 75-minute window from 12:15 to 13:30 GMT, or**8:15 to 9:30 p.m. Philippine Standard Time**. Its webcast is expected to begin about 30 minutes earlier, at roughly 7:45 p.m. PHT. The schedule can still move because this is a developmental flight. For Filipino viewers, the evening timing makes the international launch unusually accessible, while the mission’s outcome could shape the next phase of SpaceX’s reusable-rocket program.

Flight 14 would be Starship’s first attempt to reach Earth orbit. SpaceX says earlier flights used passively safe suborbital paths, while this mission is designed to insert the upper stage into orbit, complete about six circuits at roughly 275 kilometers, then perform a deorbit burn and controlled Pacific splashdown west of Chile. Space.com independently identifies it as the first orbital Starship test and reports the same earliest liftoff time and 75-minute window. The planned mission should last nearly 10 hours—far longer than previous roughly hour-long Starship test flights.

The upper stage is planned to deploy**26 Starlink V3 satellites**, SpaceX says, before reentry. The company says each satellite is designed to add 1 terabit per second of capacity, for 26 Tbps on this flight, and that the satellites could begin serving customers a few weeks after launch following orbital checkout. Those are**company projections**, not a confirmed Philippine service launch. SpaceX’s page does not name the Philippines, give a local activation date, or announce retail availability tied to Flight 14. Readers should treat the connectivity upside as a planned international network expansion, not proof that Starlink V3 is already available locally.

The test also lands in a Philippine space moment. PhilSA said September 26 that 10 Filipino engineers are training in South Korea through October with Perigee Aerospace on a liquid-fueled sounding rocket for a planned 2027 launch test in Lal-lo, Cagayan. Inquirer reported the same details on September 27. The two programs are**not the same mission**: Starship Flight 14 is a US-based SpaceX test, while PhilSA’s rocket is a future Philippine capability-building project. For Filipinos, watch the SpaceX webcast at night, but do not confuse global test plans with confirmed Philippine launches or services.
