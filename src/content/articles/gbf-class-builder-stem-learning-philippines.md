The Gokongwei Brothers Foundation is positioning teacher support as a technology and capacity-building issue, not only a classroom-infrastructure problem. Its GBF Class Builder platform gives educators free, ready-to-use instructional resources, while the foundation’s STEM Collab program provides expert-led learning sessions and a community for sharing classroom practice. Philippine Star reported that STEM Collab now has 380,000 teacher members. Together, the programs show how an education platform can extend support beyond a single school and help teachers access materials when they need them.

![GBF Class Builder reading remediation toolkit](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/gbf-class-builder-stem-learning-philippines/image-1.webp?v=20261029)

Class Builder is designed around the everyday work of teaching. The foundation describes it as a professional-development program that supports content mastery and pedagogy, while the latest report emphasizes its library of instructional resources. That combination matters in STEM subjects, where teachers may need lesson structures, demonstrations and explanations that are difficult to build from scratch. A free digital platform can reduce preparation time, but its value will depend on how well the materials match Philippine curricula, classroom conditions and the different levels of learner access.

![GBF Class Builder teaching resources and activity sheets](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/gbf-class-builder-stem-learning-philippines/image-2.webp?v=20261029)

GBF is expanding the platform alongside physical education investments. The foundation, Robinsons Land Corp. and the Department of Education broke ground on classrooms for three schools in Pangasinan and one in Bacolod. The projects respond to a national classroom shortage, while Class Builder and STEM Collab address the quality and support that teachers bring into those rooms. Linking infrastructure with teacher development is significant because connectivity, devices and digital content cannot substitute for safe learning spaces or sustained professional support.

![Teacher using the GBF Class Builder learning platform](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/gbf-class-builder-stem-learning-philippines/image-3.webp?v=20261029)

For schools considering edtech, the Philippine example highlights a practical test: technology should make good teaching easier rather than add another isolated system. Open resources, peer communities and continuous training can help platforms become part of a teacher’s routine. GBF’s next challenge will be measuring whether Class Builder improves lesson quality and learner outcomes as its reach grows. That evidence can guide future partnerships between foundations, schools and government, especially in communities where teachers have limited time, devices or access to specialist STEM training.
