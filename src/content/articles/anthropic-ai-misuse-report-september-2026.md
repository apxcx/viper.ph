**Anthropic’s September 2026 threat-intelligence report says malicious use of AI is becoming broader, faster, and more autonomous**. The company describes operations that used Claude across cyber activity, influence operations, surveillance, scams and fraud, biological misuse, conventional-weapons development, and illicit model distillation. Anthropic says it disrupted the cases, strengthened safeguards, and shared intelligence with authorities and industry partners where appropriate.

The report covers activity observed between December 2025 and August 2026. One of its central findings is that AI is moving beyond simple chatbot assistance. In several cases, actors used AI-driven workflows and multi-agent systems to automate reconnaissance, tool development, phishing infrastructure, exploitation, data processing, and exfiltration. Humans remained involved by selecting targets and reviewing results, but the systems could operate at greater speed and scale.

**For organizations in the Philippines, the warning is relevant to ordinary defensive work**. Security teams should assume that attackers may use AI to improve phishing, credential theft, malware adaptation, and the analysis of stolen data. Basic controls such as multifactor authentication, least-privilege access, patching, employee training, network monitoring, and tested incident-response plans remain important even as the tools used by attackers become more capable.

Anthropic’s report is a company-produced account of misuse involving its services, so it should be read alongside independent reporting and other threat-intelligence research. Its value is in the detailed case studies and indicators it shares, as well as the explanation of how safeguards were improved. The broader takeaway is that AI safety is increasingly tied to cybersecurity, platform governance, and cooperation among technology companies, governments, and defenders.
