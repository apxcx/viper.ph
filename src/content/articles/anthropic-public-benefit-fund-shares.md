Anthropic says it will commit a portion of its equity to a new**Anthropic Public Benefit Fund**, creating a separate vehicle for mission-focused work as the AI company grows. Subject to stockholder approval, the company plans to place 5% of its shares into the fund initially. Anthropic describes the move as an effort to dedicate more corporate value to public-good projects while preserving its existing work in AI safety, interpretability, biomedical research and discounted access for nonprofits and scientific institutions.

The fund could receive additional equity as Anthropic’s valuation rises. The company says its board may add another 2% for every 50% increase in value above a set baseline. Anthropic plans to organize the vehicle as a wholly owned limited-liability company rather than a 501(c)(3). That structure, it says, provides more flexibility for large donations or direct distributions to specific groups. The announcement does not yet provide the fund’s full governance rules, valuation baseline or a detailed schedule for future allocations.

Anthropic points to labor-market disruption as one area where the fund could act. The company says AI may create substantial economic value while also causing instability for workers, and notes a prior $200 million commitment to study and address economic-futures issues. A public-benefit fund could eventually support research, nonprofits or direct assistance, but the announcement does not promise a specific program or payout. It presents the fund as a flexible mechanism for decisions that may be difficult to fit inside a conventional corporate product roadmap.

The company also says none of the equity held by the fund will be used for lobbying or political giving in the United States or elsewhere. Combined with founder and employee donation commitments, Anthropic estimates that roughly 20% of its shares will be dedicated to public benefit, plus any milestone-based additions. The announcement is a corporate-governance commitment, not a new AI model or product launch. Its practical impact will depend on stockholder approval, final governance, future donations and the public reporting Anthropic says it will provide.
