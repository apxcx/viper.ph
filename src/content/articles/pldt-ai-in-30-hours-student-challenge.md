PLDT Enterprise, PLDT Innolab, and ePLDT used a 30-hour AI challenge to put university students in a compressed product-building environment. Participants moved from identifying a problem to developing and presenting an AI-enabled solution, with mentors and technology partners helping them make decisions under a tight deadline. The format was less about producing a finished commercial product than about exposing young Filipino innovators to the practical work behind AI: defining a useful problem, testing an idea, and explaining why a proposed system should matter.

![Students attend the PLDT AI in Action event](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/pldt-ai-in-30-hours-student-challenge/image-1.webp?v=202611270824)

Team Lord of the Pings, made up of students from De La Salle University and the Polytechnic University of the Philippines, won the competition. Team ByteLog from De La Salle University-Dasmariñas placed first runner-up, while Team BYTX from the same university finished second runner-up. The results show a competition built around applied skills rather than a single institutional pipeline. Students from several schools were able to demonstrate how technical knowledge, collaboration, and presentation can combine when the problem and the deadline are concrete.

![PLDT AI in Action event technology display](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/pldt-ai-in-30-hours-student-challenge/image-2.webp?v=202611270824)

The challenge also connected the student work to a broader Philippine AI ecosystem. Partners included De La Salle University, Animo Labs, Rokid Philippines, UiPath, 6D Technologies, Genesys, Fortinet, Microsoft, and Huawei Philippines. The event’s mentorship and knowledge-sharing sessions gave participants access to industry perspectives that classrooms cannot always provide. That bridge is important as businesses and government agencies look for people who can translate AI concepts into tools for customer service, operations, public reporting, and other real-world needs. It also gives students a clearer view of how technical choices affect users.

![PLDT AI in 30 Hours challenge panel](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/pldt-ai-in-30-hours-student-challenge/image-3.webp?v=202611270824)

PLDT Enterprise later featured the winning teams alongside local-government AI projects at its AI in Action Forum. Those public-sector examples covered zoning compliance, citizen concerns, public-service reporting, and infrastructure monitoring. Together, the initiatives frame AI adoption as a capability-building exercise, not just a software purchase. The long-term value of a 30-hour challenge will depend on what happens after the event: whether students continue building, whether institutions support responsible experimentation, and whether useful prototypes can earn a path into everyday Philippine services.
