Public concern about the**dark web**grew in the Philippines after authorities linked it to online activity being examined in the Banga National High School shooting. In a September 20 GMA News explainer, HP described the dark web as internet content absent from standard search indexes and reachable through specialized software or networks. The Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC) added that access also requires other applications or systems. The explanation describes how hidden forums can host anonymous communities and illegal trading, while avoiding the misleading suggestion that every dark-web user is a criminal.

The distinction starts with the**surface web**: publicly available news sites, social-media pages and other pages that ordinary browsers and search engines can reach. The dark web is a smaller, intentionally hidden portion of the broader deep web, and CICC executive director Undersecretary Aboy Paraiso said “deep” is only an adjective in the phrase “deep dark web.” He also cautioned that dark-web forums are difficult to penetrate. Investigators may need years of intelligence work and trusted credentials before communities allow access, so a headline about dark-web involvement is not proof of what someone viewed.

CICC said hidden forums can include hacking discussions, illegal firearms sales and communities that seek anonymity. Those possibilities explain the agency’s concern, but the GMA report does not announce a new Philippine consumer service, app or government monitoring product. A September 2025 Inquirer report separately quoted Paraiso saying CICC had the skills to access the dark web, while continuous monitoring would face budget constraints. That earlier account is context, not evidence of a newly launched 24/7 operation. Readers should treat claims about specific users, sites or criminal transactions as allegations unless investigators publicly substantiate them.

For families, CICC advised parents to pay attention to children’s online activities and changes such as unusual distance or irritability. DILG Secretary Jonvic Remulla likewise urged parents to check social-media searches and visited websites. The advice is about supervision and conversation, not a claim that Filipino children are accessing dark-web services or that a particular site caused the school shooting. Philippine availability is therefore limited to the confirmed statements and reported investigation context above.
