DOLE Secretary Francis N. Tolentino has urged Philippine employers to prepare workers for artificial intelligence before the technology changes their jobs. Speaking at the Philippine Daily Inquirer’s What Works at Work Summit 2026 in Pasay City, he said technology should strengthen human capability rather than come at the expense of jobs, rights, and dignity. The message places workforce planning alongside software adoption. It also gives businesses a practical starting point: identify which tasks may change, then prepare people for the responsibilities and skills that will remain important.

![Artificial intelligence and machine learning visual for workplace transformation](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/ai-workforce-philippines/image-1.webp?v=202611250802)

Tolentino linked the call to the Trabaho Para sa Bayan Plan 2025–2034, which prioritizes upskilling and reskilling for emerging work. He said some roles may disappear, others may be redesigned, and new occupations may develop as AI becomes more common. That framing matters for Philippine companies deciding whether to automate routine processes. Rather than treating AI as a simple cost-saving purchase, employers need to map how systems will affect existing teams, training requirements, and opportunities for workers to move into higher-value roles.

![Humans and robots representing cooperation in an AI-enabled workplace](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/ai-workforce-philippines/image-2.webp?v=202611250802)

The labor secretary also argued that human resource professionals must take a more strategic role in organizational transformation. Decisions about workforce planning, technology adoption, employee development, and training will shape whether productivity gains translate into better jobs. DOLE’s position does not reject automation; it sets a condition for it. People still provide judgment, complex problem solving, creativity, and workplace relationships even when software handles routine tasks. For companies, that means measuring AI projects by both operational performance and the quality of the transition for employees.

![AI robot and employee illustrating changing job skills](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/ai-workforce-philippines/image-3.webp?v=202611250802)

The announcement arrives as organizations globally experiment with generative AI, assistants, and automated workflows, but it does not create a new Philippine AI regulation or a deadline for adoption. It is a policy direction from DOLE and a warning against leaving workers behind. Employers that start with skills audits, transparent communication, and practical training will be better placed to test AI responsibly. The immediate question for Philippine businesses is therefore not whether change is coming, but whether their people are ready to participate in it.
