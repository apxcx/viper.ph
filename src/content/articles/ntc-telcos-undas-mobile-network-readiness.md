The National Telecommunications Commission said the country’s three major mobile operators were ready for the Undas travel rush after network tests at 12 Metro Manila bus terminals. The review matters because terminals become temporary connectivity hotspots as passengers coordinate trips, contact relatives and access travel information. NTC’s assessment covered DITO Telecommunity, Globe Telecom and Smart Communications, giving the public a rare side-by-side snapshot of network performance in crowded transport locations before the holiday movement peaked. The tests provide a useful baseline for commuters who depend on their phones throughout the holiday journey.

![telecommunications tower supporting mobile network coverage](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/ntc-telcos-undas-mobile-network-readiness/image-1.webp?v=20261102)

Nearly 90 percent of the 128 tests conducted on October 29 recorded mobile broadband download speeds of at least 50 megabits per second, according to the commission. DITO posted the highest single reading at 851 Mbps at the Parañaque Integrated Terminal Exchange, while Smart reached 456 Mbps at the same site. Globe’s highest result was 330 Mbps at the Raymond Transportation Terminal in Quezon City, showing that performance varied by operator and location. The readings also show why a single national speed ranking would hide important differences between terminals.

![cellphone tower and mobile infrastructure](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/ntc-telcos-undas-mobile-network-readiness/image-2.webp?v=20261102)

The tests also showed stronger consistency for messaging than for voice calls. All three operators achieved a 100 percent average SMS success rate, exceeding the NTC’s prescribed standard of more than 99 percent. DITO and Globe recorded zero average call-drop rates, meeting the regulator’s target of less than two percent. Smart, however, posted a 5.26 percent average call-drop rate, an issue the agency’s continuing nationwide monitoring is designed to track. That gap makes voice performance an important part of network quality even when data speeds look strong.

![large cellular tower for network capacity](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/ntc-telcos-undas-mobile-network-readiness/image-3.webp?v=20261102)

The results underline why capacity planning is becoming a core public-service responsibility for Philippine telcos. Reliable networks support more than holiday conversations: they carry digital payments, navigation, emergency coordination and access to government information. NTC said it would continue monitoring services and pursue regulatory action where necessary. For users, the practical takeaway is mixed but useful: mobile data and texting looked resilient at tested terminals, while voice reliability still deserves close attention. Those services increasingly depend on the same mobile infrastructure during periods of unusually heavy demand.
