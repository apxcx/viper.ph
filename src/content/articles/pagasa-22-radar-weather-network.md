PAGASA is planning a national network of 22 Doppler radars to give the Philippines faster and more precise weather information. Philippine Star reported that the weather bureau currently has 18 operational radars, with additional facilities planned in Pangasinan, Bataan, Ilocos and Palawan. The expansion is designed around coverage and redundancy. Overlapping radar ranges can keep monitoring active when one site is offline, an important safeguard for a country exposed to typhoons, southwest monsoon rains and rapidly changing local weather across the archipelago.

![Doppler weather radar dome used for storm research](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/pagasa-22-radar-weather-network/image-1.webp?v=20261030)

The latest project is a P250-million Doppler radar facility at Bataan Peninsula State University in Abucay. Once activated, the radar will track weather systems across Central Luzon, Metro Manila and Southern Tagalog, regions that include dense communities, major transport links and vulnerable flood zones. PAGASA expects the facility to become operational in about two years. A Subic radar that has served beyond its lifespan will eventually be decommissioned, making the new installation part of a broader modernization rather than a simple addition.

![Norman Doppler weather radar installation](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/pagasa-22-radar-weather-network/image-2.webp?v=20261030)

PAGASA also plans to make the information more useful outside the agency. Data from the Bataan facility will be made available through the PAGASA National Hydro-Met Observing Network, or PANaHON, the bureau’s weather application and observation platform. Faster radar updates can help local officials make earlier decisions about evacuation, road closures and emergency coordination. Farmers and other weather-sensitive businesses can also benefit from more localized information, especially when heavy rain or fast-moving storms create conditions that broad forecasts cannot describe in enough detail.

![Early NSSL Doppler weather radar system](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/pagasa-22-radar-weather-network/image-3.webp?v=20261030)

The radar program shows why weather technology is an infrastructure issue as much as a forecasting issue. PAGASA administrator Nathaniel Servando said the network needs enough overlap to continue operating during equipment failures, while Science Secretary Renato Solidum Jr. linked radar information to faster decisions by local chief executives. PAGASA has also deployed 11 X-Band radars for localized agricultural forecasting. The long-term test will be whether new sensors, reliable connectivity and public-facing data work together before extreme weather reaches communities that need warning time most.
