Globe AT HOME and Grab are expanding GFiber Prepaid’s order-and-install WiFi offer beyond its initial Pasig rollout. Globe’s official newsroom says the service now reaches more serviceable areas in Metro Manila, Cebu and Davao, while a same-day Philstar report details the GrabMart workflow. The development is about making broadband acquisition more app-like, not building a new network.**Availability still depends on the address and installation capacity.**Globe’s “first” order-and-install description is a company claim, while Philstar’s article is disclosed sponsored content rather than independent product testing.

Customers place the request through GrabMart inside the Grab app, then choose the GFiber Prepaid offer where serviceability allows. Globe advertises an entry price of ₱699 for seven days of unlimited internet at speeds of up to 300 Mbps. Those are package terms, not a promise that every customer receives 300 Mbps or a permanent broadband subscription. Installation is promoted as being available in as fast as two hours, but that is a conditional target.**Readers should treat speed, price, timing and coverage as advertised terms requiring checkout confirmation.**

The model connects a delivery platform with a fixed-line installation process. That could reduce the friction of a traditional application, especially for households needing short-term connectivity or a faster way to request service. It does not establish customer adoption, measured speeds, installation success rates or satisfaction. The sources also do not confirm every barangay, modem model, tax, fee or reload condition.**The useful distinction is between a simpler ordering channel and a guaranteed broadband experience.**The latter still depends on the address, network conditions and the installer’s capacity.

Prospective users should check Globe’s address-level serviceability page and the live GrabMart listing before paying. The named areas Metro Manila, Cebu and Davao should not be read as blanket coverage. Nor should the seven-day plan be compared directly with a long-term fixed broadband subscription without accounting for duration and terms.**Globe and Grab’s announcement shows how connectivity is becoming app-orderable, but the practical test remains whether a specific home can be served on the promised schedule.**No independent source reviewed here measured the offer’s actual throughput.
