The Land Transportation Office has ordered all concerned offices to stop using the legacy**STRADCOM information-technology system**and move transactions exclusively to the Land Transportation Management System, or LTMS. The directive covers vehicle registration, driver’s-license services, certificates of stock reports, and other transactions. The shift follows an Ombudsman instruction cited in the report, and it marks a major change in how motorists interact with the agency’s digital systems.

The phase-out matters because the old parallel system had been associated with additional computer-fee collections from motorists. With LTO offices instructed to rely on LTMS, the agency’s position is that official transactions should no longer require the separate STRADCOM-linked computer charge. Motorists should still ask for an official fee breakdown and receipt, because regular registration, licensing, inspection, and other statutory charges remain separate from the discontinued computer-fee issue.

Under the LTO memoranda reported by AutoIndustriya, the exclusive-LTMS instruction applies to Enhanced Driver’s License System pilot sites and 42 temporary offsite printing offices. Covered services include new vehicle registration, renewal, driver’s-license applications, renewals, replacements, changes of information, and miscellaneous transactions. Offices were directed to coordinate with the LTO central office on user access, system configuration, and technical problems during the transition.

For motorists, the immediate practical test will be whether LTMS can handle transactions consistently without delays or unofficial add-on charges. LTO’s transition guidance says technical or operational problems should be reported to its Management Information Division. Before paying, applicants should verify the transaction in LTMS, request an itemized official receipt, and report any charge described as a computer fee. The change is intended to make LTO services more orderly, but its success will depend on stable systems and clear frontline implementation.
