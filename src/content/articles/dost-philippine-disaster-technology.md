The Department of Science and Technology is expanding**operational tools for disaster readiness**in the Philippines, combining artificial intelligence, hazard data and field equipment. The effort includes the**Handa Integrated Platform for Preparedness and Response**, PAGASA forecasting experiments, the**PANaHON**weather network and a Mobile Command and Control Vehicle turned over to Tuguegarao. The Philippine Star reported the developments after a Cagayan Valley science and technology briefing. Together, the projects show a shift from publishing warnings alone toward giving agencies and communities usable information, reporting channels and deployable communications during emergencies.

Handa is the public-facing centerpiece. Launched in March and available at**handa.georisk.gov.ph**, it brings together hazard information from government agencies and uses**geospatial data and artificial intelligence**to support preparedness. Users can view alerts, locate evacuation centers, share locations and submit reports about flooding, fires, road accidents and other incidents. The Office of Civil Defense and participating local governments verify reports before they appear on the map. GMA News independently described the platform’s AI assistant, multi-hazard monitoring and role-based government dashboards, confirming that this is a**Philippine service already available for use**, not an overseas announcement.

PAGASA is testing AI for a different operational task: shortening the time needed to process large weather datasets. DOST Secretary Renato Solidum Jr. said the agency is experimenting with reducing a roughly three-hour processing cycle to about 15 minutes, with a longer-range target covering five to 14 days. That remains a**test and target**, not a claim that AI now runs all Philippine forecasts. Separately, PAGASA has released**PANaHON**, its National Hydro-Met Observing Network application, which provides near-real-time readings from weather stations around the country. Both initiatives are domestically operated, while the AI forecasting result remains unconfirmed at full scale.

The physical layer is the**MoCCoV**, or Mobile Command and Control Vehicle, turned over to the Tuguegarao local government. The unit can reportedly be set up in remote areas in about 20 minutes and includes command, conference and rescue or medical-triage spaces, plus solar panels and a backup generator rated for up to 32 hours. Philippine Information Agency reporting also confirms Tuguegarao’s mobile command center is operational, with hybrid power, weather sensors, cameras, radio equipment and satellite internet. These are**confirmed Philippine deployments**; they should not be presented as a nationwide rollout or as a guarantee of faster response in every disaster.
