Public schools in Metro Manila are receiving a fresh equipment boost as the Department of Education accepted 133 information and communications technology units through a private-sector partnership. DepEd Secretary Sonny Angara and IT-Business Process Association of the Philippines officials led the ceremonial turnover for schools in areas including Navotas and Caloocan. The donation is aimed at the practical side of digital transformation: giving learners and school staff access to working computers instead of treating connectivity and technology as abstract policy goals. For communities with limited household devices, the equipment can directly expand classroom and administrative capacity.

![DepEd official presenting a laptop during an ICT briefing](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-133-ict-units-metro-schools/image-1.webp?v=20261114073000)

The package includes 33 refurbished laptops from Etrading Software Limited Philippines and 50 desktop computers plus 50 monitors from Probe CX Philippines. IBPAP facilitated the donation and transferred ownership of the equipment to DepEd for public-school use. The mix matters because schools need different devices for different tasks. Laptops support flexible instruction and mobility, while desktop stations can anchor computer laboratories and shared work areas. The handover also shows how refurbishment can put usable technology into classrooms faster and at a lower cost than waiting for entirely new procurement cycles.

![DepEd official presenting a laptop at a digital education briefing](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-133-ict-units-metro-schools/image-2.webp?v=20261114073000)

The turnover forms part of IBPAP’s Byte the Gap initiative, which asks member companies to help narrow the digital divide in public education. Inquirer reported that the wider DepEd-IBPAP partnership has facilitated the distribution of more than 2,100 ICT units to public schools nationwide, including earlier deliveries to Taguig and Valenzuela. Scale will determine the program’s impact. Equipment must be installed, maintained, secured, and paired with teacher support so the devices become part of regular learning rather than remaining underused assets after the ceremony ends.

![Education technology partnership event with students and computers](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/deped-133-ict-units-metro-schools/image-3.webp?v=20261114073000)

The next challenge is turning donated hardware into sustained digital learning. Schools will need reliable power and connectivity, clear assignment of responsibility for repairs, and classroom plans that use the devices across subjects. Students also need guidance on online safety, digital citizenship, and practical technology skills connected to future work. Angara’s call to treat the equipment as an investment captures the core issue: access improves only when hardware is protected and integrated into teaching. Public-private partnerships can accelerate delivery, but DepEd and local school communities will determine whether the computers create lasting opportunity.
