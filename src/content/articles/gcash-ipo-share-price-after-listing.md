GCash parent Mynt Inc. ended its first week as a listed company below its initial public offering price, closing at P6.27 per share after debuting at P6.60. The move has triggered mixed interpretations rather than a single verdict on the fintech’s market value. Some analysts argue that a first-week price does not determine the performance of a business built for years of expansion. Others say the weak follow-through suggests the IPO did not attract as many new investors as expected.

![GCash logo on a blue background](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/gcash-ipo-share-price-after-listing/image-1.webp)

The debate is taking place against GCash’s substantial operating scale. Philstar cited a monthly user base of 41.5 million, while Mynt’s official investor-relations page confirms that the listed parent trades on the Philippine Stock Exchange under the ticker GCASH. The company also reported P10.82 billion in profit and P43.27 billion in revenue for the first half, according to the report. Those figures give investors a business-performance reference beyond the daily movement of the stock price. That scale remains the core story.

![GCash IPO shares promotion on the GCash app](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/gcash-ipo-share-price-after-listing/image-2.webp)

The listing is also a test of whether public-market access can help GCash expand its services and unlock value for shareholders. A lower price may make the shares more accessible to some investors, but it can also signal that the offer valuation was ambitious. The distinction between price and value is especially relevant for a fintech whose growth depends on transaction activity, credit quality, customer retention and continued trust. Public ownership adds more disclosure, market scrutiny and pressure to show that scale can become durable earnings.

![GCash mobile banking app on a smartphone](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/10/gcash-ipo-share-price-after-listing/image-3.webp)

Mynt’s official investor information says its shares can be bought or sold through licensed Philippine stockbrokers, including GStocks PH in the GCash app. That does not turn the app into a guarantee of performance, and investors still face commissions, market risk and changing valuations. For users, the listing does not immediately change how GCash payments work. For the company, however, the public debut creates a continuing scorecard: whether its user base, revenue and profit can support the expectations attached to the country’s landmark fintech IPO.
