Filipino structural engineer Aldrei Ong has won the 2026 Global Sustainability Award of the James Dyson Award for Sentinel, a concrete spacer block that doubles as a structural-health sensor. The recognition puts a Philippine invention in a global engineering spotlight while pointing to a practical safety problem: after an earthquake, cracks may be hidden inside concrete long before they become visible. Sentinel is designed to help engineers identify those changes earlier, using a construction component that buildings already need rather than adding a costly standalone device.

![Sentinel piezoresistive effect diagram](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/filipino-engineer-sentinel-earthquake-sensor/image-1.webp)

The idea is deliberately simple. Ong replaces a conventional spacer block with self-sensing concrete, a mix that changes its electrical resistance when the material is stressed or cracked. Electrodes embedded in the block connect to a small electronic module that reads the signal against a baseline. A meaningful change can then be sent wirelessly to a monitoring dashboard, giving engineers a remote indication that a structure needs closer inspection. The result does not replace professional assessment, but it could help teams prioritize buildings after earthquakes or during long-term deterioration.

![Sentinel design iterations for concrete sensing](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/filipino-engineer-sentinel-earthquake-sensor/image-2.webp)

That distinction matters in the Philippines, where a major earthquake could leave homes, schools, hospitals and churches waiting for clearance at the same time. Visual inspections and specialist tests remain essential, yet they can be slow when access is unsafe or resources are stretched. The James Dyson Award project page says Sentinel can cost about PHP10 per block at manufacture, a target that could support wider deployment if field validation confirms durability and accuracy. The technology’s strongest argument is therefore not novelty alone, but the possibility of embedding an early-warning layer into ordinary construction.

![Sentinel research presentation and engineering development](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/filipino-engineer-sentinel-earthquake-sensor/image-3.webp)

Sentinel has already moved beyond a classroom concept. The project page says sensors have been embedded in a Philippine building for real-world monitoring, while Ong plans to improve the electronics with a custom printed circuit board and develop versions for existing structures. For property owners and public agencies, the next questions are evidence, maintenance and integration with engineering workflows. The award provides funding and visibility, but adoption will depend on independent testing, clear alert thresholds and responsible interpretation. If those pieces align, a small block hidden inside concrete could become a useful part of safer Philippine buildings.
