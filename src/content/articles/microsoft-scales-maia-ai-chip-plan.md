Microsoft says its custom Azure Maia chips are moving from experimentation into large-scale production. In an Oct. 12 announcement, the company said Maia 200 is already serving Microsoft workloads, including Copilot, while Maia 300 is scheduled for production deployments in 2027. Microsoft plans to scale the Maia family to multi-gigawatt deployments and make it a primary inference system for its products and services. The strategy shows how cloud companies are designing silicon, software and data-center systems together to control AI performance and cost.

Maia 300 is designed for long-context reasoning, latency-sensitive conversations and sustained multi-step agentic tasks. Microsoft expects it to deliver 40 percent more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems. In a full 96-accelerator configuration, the company says it can provide up to seven times the rack-scale token throughput of Maia 200. These are Microsoft estimates, not independent benchmark results, but they show the commercial pressure to serve more AI interactions within each available unit of power for cloud services and enterprise workloads.

The system uses eight HBM4 stacks with up to 18.0 terabytes per second of peak memory bandwidth and a 950-watt thermal design power, according to Microsoft. Its chiplet architecture separates compute, memory-controller and input-output functions, while TSMC’s N2P process is used for compute chiplets. Microsoft also highlights software-managed SRAM, low-precision formats and an AI-optimized Ethernet transport. The design targets inference workloads where moving data efficiently can be as important as adding raw computing power in every generation at scale.

Microsoft says Maia 200 is deploying across the United States, Europe and Australia, delivering up to 1.7 times more tokens per watt for selected Teams, Copilot and Cowork capabilities than leading GPU infrastructure. Maia 300 is intended to support Microsoft Foundry, GitHub Copilot and future model-serving workloads. For the wider AI market, the announcement reinforces a shift toward specialized accelerators, tighter hardware-software co-design and energy efficiency as demand for inference continues to grow across global markets for cloud operators.
