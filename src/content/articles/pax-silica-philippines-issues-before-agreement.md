The Philippines is not yet moving to a Pax Silica agreement after the United States urged the government to resolve outstanding concerns first. Senate President Sherwin Gatchalian said US Secretary of State Marco Rubio told him that the country should have time to address issues surrounding the initiative. The message matters because Pax Silica is tied to an ambitious technology and supply-chain agenda, but it is not a completed investment contract. The latest development keeps the proposal in the policy stage while Philippine officials and affected groups continue debating its terms and consequences.

![Officials displaying signed Pax Silica cooperation documents beside national flags](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/pax-silica-philippines-issues-before-agreement/image-1.webp?v=202611231156)

There was no signing during US President Donald Trump’s visit to Manila, according to statements cited by Inquirer. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. and US Ambassador Lee Lipton both said the initiative was not part of the trip, while Trump referred questions to the ambassador. That distinction is important for readers following announcements about a proposed high-tech manufacturing and semiconductor hub in New Clark City. The Philippines remains involved in the wider initiative, but the diplomatic and domestic conditions needed for a formal agreement have not been completed.

![Pax Silica branding displayed at a strategic technology event](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/pax-silica-philippines-issues-before-agreement/image-2.webp?v=202611231156)

Pax Silica aims to strengthen supply chains for semiconductors, artificial intelligence infrastructure, critical minerals and other strategic technologies while reducing dependence on China. For the Philippines, the potential upside includes investment, technical work and a larger role in high-value manufacturing. The proposal also raises questions about electricity, land, logistics, environmental safeguards, labor and how benefits would reach local communities. Those questions are not side issues for a technology hub. They determine whether infrastructure can operate reliably and whether the country can capture lasting value instead of only hosting facilities.

![US Department of State fact sheet explaining the Pax Silica Initiative](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/pax-silica-philippines-issues-before-agreement/image-3.webp?v=202611231156)

Inquirer reported that farmers, Indigenous communities, environmental groups and other sectors have criticized parts of the proposal, including its connection to the planned high-tech hub in Tarlac. Gatchalian said concerns involving Pax Silica and the Luzon Economic Corridor should be addressed before the Philippines moves forward. The practical next step is therefore clarification rather than construction: officials must explain the agreement, consult affected communities and set enforceable protections. Until that work is done, headlines should describe Pax Silica as a developing policy initiative, not a signed deal or confirmed data-center build.
