Globe Telecom reported a 96-percent surge in theft-related incidents affecting its network infrastructure through October 2026. Inquirer Business said the company recorded 2,616 pilferage-related incident tickets from January to October, compared with 1,334 during the same period last year. The stolen equipment includes fiber-optic cables, backup batteries, transmission hardware, fuel, and electrical components. The increase is more than a corporate loss report. It shows how physical security can become a technology issue when damaged infrastructure interrupts mobile service, broadband access, and business connectivity across Philippine communities.

![Philippine internet infrastructure map showing cable routes and network exposure](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/globe-network-theft/image-1.webp?v=202611240600)

Globe said the incidents have been recorded across the country, with 361 tickets reported in the Visayas alone. The company linked the thefts to localized outages and weaker service reliability for consumers and enterprises. Each missing cable, battery, or generator component can also divert money from planned network expansion into emergency repairs and replacement work. That trade-off matters as Philippine users depend on connectivity for payments, remote work, public services, education, and commerce. A network can be technically capable yet still fail when physical assets are repeatedly removed or damaged.

![Fiber connectivity map representing the national network supporting Philippine users](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/globe-network-theft/image-2.webp?v=202611240600)

The company also pointed to recent enforcement results. Globe’s security unit and local law-enforcement partners helped secure four convictions in Cebu City and Carcar City for copper-cable theft, while two arrests in Bacolod involved the alleged theft of 290 meters of cable. The cases fall under the Revised Penal Code and Anti-Fencing Law. Those outcomes do not solve the wider problem, but they demonstrate why telecom operators need evidence collection, coordinated investigations, and faster local response. Protecting network infrastructure requires both engineering controls and consistent prosecution.

![International cable routes illustrating the infrastructure behind internet access](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/globe-network-theft/image-3.webp?v=202611240600)

Globe is calling for stronger coordination among the National Telecommunications Commission, the Department of the Interior and Local Government, the Philippine National Police, and local governments. The request places telecom equipment within the broader category of critical infrastructure that communities and the economy rely on daily. Better reporting can help identify theft patterns, while local governments can improve site security and scrap-metal enforcement. For customers, the immediate lesson is that outages are not always caused by capacity or weather. In some cases, service reliability depends on whether physical network assets remain in place.
