The**Philippine National Police**is tightening security on the ground and in cyberspace ahead of the 49th ASEAN Summit and Related Summits, scheduled for November 16 to 18 in Metro Manila. PNP Chief Gen. Jose Melencio Nartatez Jr. directed the National Capital Region Police Office and other units to strengthen contingency planning around venues, hotels, airports and major transport hubs. The move combines conventional public-safety work with digital monitoring as the country prepares to host foreign leaders, delegates and support teams.

According to the Philippine News Agency, the cyber component will monitor**misinformation, disinformation and potential online threats**that could affect the summit. The report did not identify a specific attack, breach or threat actor. The PNP has not announced a specific incident. Instead, the PNP described the preparations as precautionary and intended to improve readiness without causing unnecessary alarm. The agency said the work follows guidance from President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. through Interior Secretary Jonvic Remulla, with police units coordinating with government agencies and security partners.

NCRPO preparations include increased foot and mobile patrols, area and route security, intelligence and information monitoring, protection of critical and vital installations, rapid-response capability and coordination with traffic authorities. Planning will cover summit venues, hotels, transport routes and convergence points that delegates or members of the public may use. The PNP and NCRPO also said they are coordinating with the Armed Forces of the Philippines, local governments and other law-enforcement bodies so official convoys can move while major roads remain usable for commuters and emergency responders.

For Metro Manila residents, the announcement signals an operational security build-up rather than a new public cyber-alert system. The Palace and PNP have said preparations are meant to protect delegates and the public while respecting peaceful assembly. President Marcos has declared November 16 to 18 special non-working days in the National Capital Region to ease traffic and support security measures. Authorities have not announced a general shutdown of online services, and the PNP’s statement should not be read as evidence of a confirmed cyberattack against the summit.
