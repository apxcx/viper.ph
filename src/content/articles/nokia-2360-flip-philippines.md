Nokia is bringing the 2360 Flip to the Philippines at PHP2,790, reviving a compact phone format that puts calls, texts and physical buttons ahead of app-heavy smartphone features. Inquirer Technology reports that the device has a 1.77-inch external screen and a 2.8-inch QVGA main display. Its most notable pitch is safety: a dedicated SOS button is intended to make emergency contact easier, especially for senior users who may prefer a simpler handset with fewer menus and distractions.

![Nokia flip phone with television receiver](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/nokia-2360-flip-philippines/image-1.webp)

Pressing the SOS button for three seconds automatically calls a selected contact and sends text messages to five others, according to the report. The Family Assist app adds a remote-support layer, allowing family members to set reminders, manage contacts and adjust volume through SMS. That approach is different from the companion apps built around smartphones because the phone itself remains deliberately basic. For households, the appeal is less about replacing a primary mobile device and more about giving an older relative a direct, familiar way to reach help.

![Nokia 3650 front and back reference](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/nokia-2360-flip-philippines/image-2.webp)

The 2360 Flip keeps several old-school features that have largely disappeared from modern phones: a physical keypad, loudspeaker, FM radio, 3.5mm headphone jack and removable 1,450mAh battery. HMD lists up to 27.5 hours of talk time and 15 days of standby, though real-world results will depend on coverage and usage. The trade-off is equally clear. The handset has only 4MB of RAM, 4MB of internal storage and a QVGA rear camera, with microSD expansion up to 32GB for basic files.

![Nokia 6102i Cingular reference](https://viper.ph/api/media/articles/2026/11/nokia-2360-flip-philippines/image-3.webp)

Connectivity may be the most important question for Philippine buyers. Inquirer Technology says the phone supports only 2G networks, so availability and quality of legacy service will determine whether its emergency features are dependable in a particular area. The device is therefore best understood as a focused safety and communication tool, not a smartphone substitute. Its launch shows there is still room for phones that solve one job plainly, provided buyers check network support, battery expectations and the emergency-contact setup before handing it to a family member.
